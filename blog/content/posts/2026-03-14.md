## Frontier Models, Long Context, and Agentic Retrieval

- Anthropic removed the surcharge for prompts above 200k tokens, making the full 1M‑token context window standard-priced for Claude Opus 4.6 ($5/$25 per M input/output tokens) and Sonnet 4.6 ($3/$15), and raised media limits from 100 to 600 images/PDF pages per request across Claude Code Max/Team/Enterprise and major clouds. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-drops-the-surcharge-for-million-token-context-windows-making-opus-4-6-and-sonnet-4-6-far-cheaper/), [Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought), [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/13/1m-context/#atom-everything)) — Anthropic now undercuts OpenAI and Gemini, which still charge premiums beyond ~200–270k tokens

- Anthropic reports Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 achieve the highest accuracy among peers at full 1M‑token context, including 78.3% on MRCR v2 at 1M tokens, though context-rot remains unsolved. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-drops-the-surcharge-for-million-token-context-windows-making-opus-4-6-and-sonnet-4-6-far-cheaper/), [Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

- Latent.Space argues 1M‑token windows are hitting hard physical limits due to global DRAM/HBM shortages and predicts context will not “meaningfully” exceed 1M tokens in the next two years, implying a future of “context rationing” where ultra‑long windows are premium features. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

- NVIDIA’s NeMo Retriever team unveiled an agentic retrieval pipeline (ReACT-style loop with think/retrieve/final_results tools) that achieved #1 on ViDoRe v3 (NDCG@10 69.22 with Claude Opus 4.5 + nemotron‑colembed‑vl‑8b‑v2) and #2 on the reasoning‑heavy BRIGHT benchmark (50.9 with Opus 4.5 + llama‑embed‑nemotron‑reasoning‑3b). ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemo-retriever-agentic-retrieval))

- To cut latency and deployment friction, NeMo replaced an MCP-based retriever server with an in‑process thread‑safe singleton retriever, eliminating network overhead while still sharing a GPU‑resident index across concurrent agent threads. ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemo-retriever-agentic-retrieval))

- NeMo ablations show open‑weights gpt‑oss‑120B agents trail Opus 4.5 by ~3 NDCG points on ViDoRe and ~9 on BRIGHT, and that agentic loops narrow the performance gap between strong and weak embedding models at the cost of large per‑query token and time budgets (e.g., ~136 seconds and ~760k input tokens/query on ViDoRe). ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemo-retriever-agentic-retrieval))

- OpenAI launched GPT‑5.4 across ChatGPT, Codex, and its API stack, and is already being benchmarked on tasks like proof verification where it currently rejects only ~40% of subtly false mathematical claims on the BrokenArXiv testbed. ([AI Safety Newsletter](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-69-department), [Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

## Chips, Compute Infrastructure, and Memory Bottlenecks

- SemiAnalysis projects that by 2027, AI accelerators will consume ~86% of TSMC’s N3 output, up from just under 60% in 2026, as Nvidia’s Rubin, Google’s TPU v7/v8, Amazon’s Trainium3, and AMD’s MI350X all converge on N3 and TSMC struggles to expand capacity fast enough. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-chips-are-pushing-everything-else-off-tsmcs-most-advanced-production-lines/))

- TSMC’s N3 utilization is expected to exceed 100% in 2H26, with the foundry already offloading certain process layers to other fabs and redirecting wafer starts away from weakening smartphone demand, which now acts as a “release valve” that can be sacrificed to make room for more AI wafers. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-chips-are-pushing-everything-else-off-tsmcs-most-advanced-production-lines/))

- HBM is an independent chokepoint: HBM currently uses ~3× as much wafer capacity per bit as standard DRAM, a ratio projected to approach 4× with HBM4, so more N3 logic capacity alone cannot resolve AI compute shortages. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-chips-are-pushing-everything-else-off-tsmcs-most-advanced-production-lines/))

- Nvidia has reportedly ceased production of H200 chips destined for China and shifted TSMC capacity toward newer Vera Rubin GPUs, while Microsoft says Azure is the first cloud to validate Nvidia’s Rubin NVL72 systems, and Lambda advocates bare‑metal Rubin clusters over hypervisors. ([AI Safety Newsletter](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-69-department), [Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

- IndexCache, a DeepSeek sparse-attention optimization highlighted by Latent.Space, reuses sparse-attention index information across layers to deliver ~1.2× end‑to‑end speedups on the 744B‑parameter GLM‑5 and up to 1.82× prefill and 1.48× decode speedups on a 30B model at 200k context with minimal code changes. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

- Reuters reported on new ASML lithography technology that could increase chip production by ~50% by 2030, a key medium‑term lever for easing supply constraints if realized. ([AI Safety Newsletter](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-69-department))

([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/hume-ai-open-sources-tada-a-speech-model-five-times-faster-than-rivals-with-zero-hallucinated-words/))

- Hermes Agent (Nous) gained traction as a self‑hostable, memory‑centric agent framework that accumulates user‑specific skills over time, while Genspark’s Claw and Perplexity Computer position remote cloud computers plus agents as persistent “AI employees” orchestrated across devices. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

- Perplexity Computer rolled out to iOS with cross‑device sync, letting users start and manage browser‑computer tasks from phones or desktops, and Claude Code added similar cross‑device session orchestration. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

## Research, Evaluation, and Data Practices

- A new “RandOpt / Neural Thickets” post‑training result claims that adding Gaussian noise to pretrained model weights and ensembling can match or outperform RL fine‑tuning (GRPO/PPO) on reasoning, coding, writing, chemistry, and VLM tasks, suggesting large models inhabit neighborhoods dense with specialized “task experts” that are easy to discover. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

- Stanford work on generic data replay finds that re‑surfacing high‑quality generic data during fine‑tuning can improve training efficiency by 1.87× (fine‑tuning) and 2.06× (mid‑training), with concrete gains like +4.5% on agentic web navigation and +2% on Basque QA, fueling renewed interest in “pre‑pre‑training” and mixture design. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-context-drought))

- The BrokenArXiv benchmark shows GPT‑5.4 rejects only ~40% of perturbed false statements from recent math papers, and MADQA finds QA agents reach near‑human accuracy largely via brute‑force document search while still leaving ~20% headroom to oracle performance, highlighting persistent evaluation gaps in truthfulness and search strategy. - Amusepri (AmShepli), from Sumitomo Pharma and Racthera, injects dopamine‑neuron progenitor cells made from donor iPS cells into the brain via minimally invasive stereotactic surgery; in six analyzed Parkinson’s patients, four improved their “off‑time” motor scores 24 months after treatment, and transplanted cells remained viable at the injection sites. ([WIRED Japan](https://www.wired.com/story/japan-approves-the-worlds-first-treatment-made-with-reprogrammed-human-cells/))

- Both therapies are manufactured at SMaRT in Osaka, the world’s first commercial plant dedicated to iPS‑derived regenerative products, using Kyoto University’s iPS cell stock and technologies from multiple industry‑academic partners; approvals require extensive post‑marketing studies (e.g., ReHeart in 75 patients) before full insurance coverage.
