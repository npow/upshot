## Custom AI hardware and large‑model infrastructure

- Meta announced four generations of its in‑house Meta Training and Inference Accelerators (MTIA 300/400/450/500), built with Broadcom on RISC‑V and fabbed by TSMC, scaling HBM bandwidth from 6.1 to 27.6 TB/s and FP8/MX8 compute from 1.2 to 10 PFLOPs, with MTIA 300 already training ranking/recommendation models and the gen‑AI‑oriented 450/500 slated for mass production in 2027. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/meta-unveils-four-generations-of-custom-ai-chips-to-cut-inference-costs-for-billions-of-users/), [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/meta-unveils-four-new-chips-to-power-its-ai-and-recommendation-systems/))

- MTIA 400, which Meta says is competitive with leading commercial accelerators, uses 72‑chip scale‑up domains, supports low‑precision MX4/MX8 formats, integrates with PyTorch, vLLM, and Triton so models can run on MTIA and GPUs without code changes, but Meta is still signing multibillion‑dollar GPU deals with NVIDIA, AMD, and Google, underscoring how hard full silicon independence remains. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/meta-unveils-four-generations-of-custom-ai-chips-to-cut-inference-costs-for-billions-of-users/), [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/meta-unveils-four-new-chips-to-power-its-ai-and-recommendation-systems/))

- NVIDIA’s Nemotron 3 Super is an open 120B‑parameter, ~12B‑active hybrid Mamba‑Transformer Latent MoE model with 1M context and multi‑token prediction that delivers up to 2.2× faster FP4 inference and ~10% higher throughput per GPU than GPT‑OSS‑120B, while its more compact KV cache (~8 KB/token BF16) makes long‑context serving lighter than Qwen3.5‑122B, and it shipped with immediate support across vLLM, llama.cpp, Ollama and major hosted providers. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-replit-agent-4-the-knowledge))

- Frontier multimodal architectures continue to converge on hybrid attention and MoE: a community breakdown of Qwen3.5 details a 397B A17B MoE and 27B dense variant with 262k–1M context, Gated DeltaNet + softmax hybrid attention, grouped query attention, dual‑stream attention, and multi‑token prediction, illustrating the emerging design template for high‑capacity, long‑context models. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-replit-agent-4-the-knowledge))


## Multimodal embeddings and retrieval

- Google’s Gemini Embedding 2 is its first native multimodal embedding model, mapping text (up to 8,192 tokens), up to six images, 120‑second videos, audio, and six‑page PDFs into a unified 3,072‑dimensional semantic space (with Matryoshka variants at 1,536 and 768 dims) and supporting interleaved multimodal inputs, with benchmarks showing clear wins over Amazon Nova 2 and Voyage Multimodal 3.5, especially on text–video retrieval. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-unifies-text-image-video-and-audio-in-a-single-vector-space-with-gemini-embedding-2/), [AI Collective](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-builders-issue-claude-code-review))

- The model processes audio natively rather than via speech‑to‑text, is available via the Gemini API and Vertex AI, and integrates with major vector DB and RAG frameworks (LangChain, LlamaIndex, Haystack, Weaviate, Qdrant, Chroma, Vertex Vector Search), though early analysis suggests its text‑only pricing is best suited to multimodal-heavy use cases. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-unifies-text-image-video-and-audio-in-a-single-vector-space-with-gemini-embedding-2/))

- Perplexity’s open‑source pplx‑embed‑v1 and pplx‑embed‑context‑v1, released earlier under an MIT license, remain notable as extremely memory‑efficient text embeddings that match Alibaba’s Qwen3 and outperform Google’s gemini‑embedding‑001 on MTEB retrieval while using significantly less memory, offering a strong open alternative for text‑only applications. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-unifies-text-image-video-and-audio-in-a-single-vector-space-with-gemini-embedding-2/))

- AgentIR, a “reasoning‑aware” retriever that jointly embeds queries with an agent’s reasoning trace, boosts accuracy on the challenging BrowseComp‑Plus benchmark to 68% versus 52% for larger generic embeddings and 37% for BM25, indicating that incorporating reasoning context into embeddings can materially improve tool‑using agents. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-replit-agent-4-the-knowledge))


## Agentic systems, orchestration, and evaluation

- NVIDIA’s AI‑Q deep researcher, built on NeMo Agent Toolkit and fine‑tuned Nemotron‑3‑Super‑120B‑A12B models, achieved first place on both DeepResearch Bench I (55.95) and II (54.50) using a multi‑agent orchestrator–planner–researcher architecture, specialist sub‑agents, long‑horizon tool‑use middleware, and an optional ensemble/refiner layer trained on ~67k quality‑filtered research trajectories. ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/how-nvidia-won-deepresearch-bench))

- IBM and Georgia Tech’s Agentic Control Center for Data Product Optimization runs specialized agents (planner, input‑planner, question generator, text‑to‑SQL, clustering, view creation) in a continuous loop against explicit “quality contracts” (coverage, complexity, speed, accuracy), with event‑driven metric recalculation and dashboards to keep human operators in the loop. ([arXiv:2603.10133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10133))

- Replit Agent 4, Perplexity “Personal Computer,” and Base44 Superagents all move beyond chat UIs toward persistent, multi‑agent runtimes: Replit offers a collaborative canvas with parallel agents for apps/sites/slides, Perplexity runs an always‑on local/cloud hybrid agent on a Mac mini that can act across local files, apps, and browser sessions, and Base44 focuses on non‑technical users with pre‑wired Gmail/Slack/Stripe/CRM integrations. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-replit-agent-4-the-knowledge))

- LangChain added autonomous context compression to its Deep Agents so models compact context at task boundaries rather than fixed token limits, and NVIDIA’s upcoming GTC talks will emphasize eval‑driven design and self‑coding agents, signaling that agent harnesses, tooling, and evaluation loops are becoming as important as the base models themselves. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-replit-agent-4-the-knowledge), [Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/how-nvidia-won-deepresearch-bench))

- New evaluation and learning work for agents includes PostTrainBench, a benchmark for testing whether agents can post‑train models in simplified R&D settings (showing that excessive “high‑effort” reasoning can hurt performance via context compaction), EvoSkill, which evolves reusable skills via executor/proposer/skill‑builder triads, and research framing reliability failures (not just adversarial attacks) as a core security problem for AI agents. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-replit-agent-4-the-knowledge))

- A KDnuggets overview highlights LangGraph, CrewAI, Pydantic AI, Google’s Agent Development Kit, AutoGen, Semantic Kernel, and LlamaIndex Agent Workflow as leading orchestration frameworks, each offering different trade‑offs in graph‑based control flow, role‑based teams, type‑safe execution, cloud‑native observability, and event‑driven async workflows for production multi‑agent systems. ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/top-7-ai-agent-orchestration-frameworks))

## LLM safety, unlearning, and reasoning reliability

- Targeted Reasoning Unlearning (TRU) augments gradient‑ascent unlearning with “reasoning‑based” targets—synthetic traces plus explicit refusal responses—so that models can learn a precise harmful content scope and how to respond coherently to in‑scope queries, achieving stronger knowledge removal and better retention on out‑of‑scope tasks than prior GA‑based methods across multiple benchmarks and attack scenarios. ([arXiv:2603.09980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09980))

- HEAL (Hindsight Entropy‑Assisted Learning) tackles the “teacher ceiling” in reasoning distillation by detecting entropy spikes as reasoning dead‑ends, injecting targeted answer‑conditioned hints to repair trajectories (GEAR), filtering out shortcut paths with a Perplexity‑Uncertainty Ratio Estimator (PURE), and organizing training via a progressive curriculum (PACE), allowing students to learn from hard problems their teacher could not solve unaided. ([arXiv:2603.10359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10359))

- TRACED (Topological Reasoning Assessment via Curvature Evolution and Displacement Dynamics) analyzes chains‑of‑thought as trajectories in a metric induced by the unembedding matrix, showing that correct reasoning traces have high cumulative displacement and low curvature change (stable progress), while hallucinated ones show low displacement and high curvature (“hesitation loops”), and uses these kinematic features in a Bayesian model that robustly discriminates correct vs. incorrect reasoning across math, science, and social reasoning benchmarks. ([arXiv:2603.10384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10384))

- Personalized Group Relative Policy Optimization (P‑GRPO) generalizes GRPO to heterogeneous preferences by normalizing advantages against cluster‑specific reward histories instead of per‑batch rewards, preventing rewards from minority or noisier preference groups from being shrunk toward majority baselines and yielding faster convergence and higher rewards in preference‑conditioned tasks on Qwen3 and Gemma models, while maintaining strong MMLU scores. ([arXiv:2603.10009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10009))

- A calibration study across 24,000 trials on MMLU, TriviaQA, ARC, and HellaSwag finds that the lowest‑performing model, Kimi K2 (23.3% accuracy), is severely overconfident (mean 95.7% confidence, ECE 0.726), while Claude Haiku 4.5 shows the best calibration (ECE 0.122) and sometimes healthy underconfidence, confirming a Dunning‑Kruger‑like pattern where weaker models overestimate their competence. ([arXiv:2603.09985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09985))

- A new medical textbook‑grounded QA benchmark shows LLaMA‑70B‑Instruct hallucinating in 19.7% of answers despite being given the relevant passage (98.8% of responses rated maximally plausible), and in a broader eight‑model comparison, lower hallucination rates strongly correlate (ρ ≈ −0.71) with clinicians’ usefulness ratings, underscoring that high benchmark accuracy can mask dangerous hallucination behavior in clinical settings. ([arXiv:2603.09986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09986))

## Domain‑specific and efficiency‑focused models

- LWM‑Temporal extends the Large Wireless Models family with Sparse Spatio‑Temporal Attention (SSTA) over angle‑delay‑time representations, restricting Transformer attention to physically plausible neighborhoods and temporal “corridors,” and is pretrained via physics‑informed masking to emulate occlusions and pilot sparsity, yielding better long‑horizon channel prediction under varying mobility and scarce fine‑tuning data. ([arXiv:2603.10024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10024))

- MoE‑SpAc reframes speculative decoding as an informative “lookahead sensor” for Mixture‑of‑Experts models on heterogeneous CPU/GPU setups, using speculative token‑frequency maps to estimate expert utility, dynamically partition experts via online integer optimization, and unify prefetch/eviction in an asynchronous engine, obtaining 42% higher tokens‑per‑second than the best speculative‑decoding baseline and about 4.04× speedup over standard offloading strategies. ([arXiv:2603.09983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09983))

- Reka Edge targets physical AI with a production‑oriented vision‑language model optimized for image/video understanding, object detection, and tool use, claiming 3× fewer input tokens and 65% faster throughput than leading 8B models on visual tasks, with a focus on robotics and embodied agents. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-replit-agent-4-the-knowledge))

- Detailed community analyses of Qwen3.5 and related models reinforce a trend toward hybrid attention (Gated DeltaNet + softmax), large sparse MoE backbones with a few billion active parameters per token, long native context windows extended toward 1M tokens, and multi‑token prediction during training and inference for both quality and speed gains. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-replit-agent-4-the-knowledge))


## Arabic and multilingual language modeling

- In the AbjadMed shared task on 82‑class Arabic medical query classification with extreme class imbalance and label noise, Georgia Tech shows a fine‑tuned AraBERTv2 encoder with hybrid mean+attention pooling, multi‑sample dropout, label smoothing, and layer‑wise learning rate decay achieves the best Macro‑F1 (0.3934), while large causal decoders like Llama‑3.3‑70B used for zero‑shot re‑ranking and Qwen‑3 3B for feature extraction underperform due to sequence‑biased embeddings and schema mismatch. ([arXiv:2603.10008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10008))

- For the AbjadGenEval Arabic AI‑generated text detection shared task, a multilingual E5‑large encoder fine‑tuned with simple mean pooling reaches an F1 of 0.75 on balanced human vs. machine data, outperforming more complex pooling strategies (weighted layer pooling, multi‑head attention, gated fusion) that overfit the small dataset; the authors note human texts are roughly twice as long as machine ones (632 vs. 303 words). ([arXiv:2603.10007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10007))

- AraModernBERT adapts the ModernBERT encoder architecture to Arabic with a 50k‑token Arabic BPE tokenizer, transtokenized embedding initialization from English models using parallel corpora, and native 8,192‑token context via alternating local/global attention and RoPE, significantly improving masked‑LM perplexity over non‑transtokenized baselines and transferring well to Arabic NLI, offensive language detection, question similarity, NER, and retrieval. ([arXiv:2603.09982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09982))

([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/grammarlys-ai-writing-tips-claim-inspiration-from-experts-who-never-agreed-to-participate/), [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/grammarly-is-facing-a-class-action-lawsuit-over-its-ai-expert-review-feature/))

## Games, edge AI, and resource‑constrained decision making

- A new framework for the Game of the Amazons integrates a Graph Attention Autoencoder, a multi‑step Monte Carlo Tree Search, a stochastic graph genetic algorithm, and GPT‑4o‑mini–generated synthetic data to learn from noisy, non‑expert supervision; on 10×10 boards the resulting lightweight agent achieves a 45% win rate vs. its teacher at 30 search nodes and 66.5% at 50 nodes, demonstrating weak‑to‑strong generalization under tight computation budgets. ([arXiv:2603.10512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10512))

- The framework uses graph attention as a structural filter to denoise LLM‑suggested moves, a staged MCTS to avoid exponential search blowup, and a stochastic graph GA to optimize evaluation signals, pointing to a more interpretable alternative to deep RL for complex board games in resource‑constrained environments. ([arXiv:2603.10512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10512))

- KDnuggets’ PersonaPlex tutorial shows how full‑duplex, real‑time, local speech‑to‑speech agents can be run on commodity Linux machines—including setup of NVIDIA’s personaplex repo, Opus audio dependencies, and a Moshi‑based web server—offering a practical blueprint for low‑latency voice interfaces that could front‑end tool‑using agent systems. ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/run-a-real-time-speech-to-speech-ai-model-locally))


## AI for data products and physical engineering

- IBM’s Agentic Control Center treats data products as continuously improvable assets: a State Manager tracks schemas, questions, SQL versions, and views; a Metric module computes quality metrics (coverage, complexity, latency); a Tool Registry links specialist agents to metrics; and an orchestration layer iteratively plans, executes, and refines actions (e.g., generating questions, SQL, views) to meet user‑defined quality contracts with Git‑backed traceability. ([arXiv:2603.10133](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10133))

- MIT Technology Review’s sponsored report emphasizes that in physical systems—from cars and appliances to medical devices—engineering teams are adopting AI cautiously via layered systems with explicit trust thresholds, focusing on predictive analytics and simulation/validation where performance can be audited and regulatory approval obtained, and prioritizing real‑world outcomes like defect rates and emissions over internal metrics or headline‑grabbing “AI transformation” narratives. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/12/1133675/pragmatic-by-design-engineering-ai-for-the-real-world/))
