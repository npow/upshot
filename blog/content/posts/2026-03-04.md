## Frontier & Open Models

- **Google’s Gemini 3.1 Flash‑Lite preview**  
  - Gemini 3.1 Flash‑Lite is now Google’s fastest, most cost‑efficient Gemini 3 model, offering a 1M‑token context window, multimodal input (text, image, video, audio, PDFs), four “thinking levels,” and >360 output tokens/s with ~5.1s average latency—about 2.5× faster time‑to‑first‑token and 45% higher throughput than Gemini 2.5 Flash at similar or better quality. ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-3-1-flash-lite-built-for-intelligence-at-scale), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-fastest-and-cheapest-model-gemini-3-1-flash-lite-got-smarter-but-also-tripled-the-price/))  
  - On Artificial Analysis benchmarks it scores 34 on the Intelligence Index (+12 vs 2.5 Flash‑Lite), 86.9% on GPQA Diamond, 76.8% on MMMU‑Pro and 72.0% on LiveCodeBench, beating previous‑gen Gemini 2.5 Flash and many peers including Claude 4.5 Haiku and Grok 4.1 Fast on multimodal reasoning and code. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/googles-fastest-and-cheapest-model-gemini-3-1-flash-lite-got-smarter-but-also-tripled-the-price/))  
  - Pricing is $0.25/M input and $1.50/M output tokens—tripling output cost vs Gemini 2.5 Flash‑Lite ($0.40/M) but still cheaper than larger competitors and 1/8 the output price of Gemini 3.1 Pro; it’s available in Google AI Studio and Vertex AI. ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-3-1-flash-lite-built-for-intelligence-at-scale), [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/3/gemini-31-flash-lite/#atom-everything))  

- **Qwen 3.5: full‑stack open‑weights push**  
  - Alibaba’s Qwen 3.5 family spans tiny on‑device models (0.8B–9B), dense 0.8B–27B models and MoE models from 35B‑A3B up to a 397B‑A17B flagship, all multimodal and “reasoning‑by‑default,” built on a Qwen‑Next architecture with Gated DeltaNet‑style hybrid attention to extend context to ~262k–1M tokens while keeping compute low. ([Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-19-qwen-35), [The Sequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-ai-of-the-week-818-you), [Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  
  - Benchmarks reported by the team and early testers show Qwen3.5‑35B‑A3B and 397B‑A17B trading blows with proprietary GPT‑5.2 and Claude Opus 4.5 on a wide range of reasoning tasks, while the 35B‑A3B “medium” and 27B dense models beat prior Qwen 3‑235B variants, especially on complex agent tasks and multimodal workloads. ([Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-19-qwen-35), [Turing Post](https://turingpost.substack.com/p/fod142-what-is-agentic-rl-and-why))  
  - The new “small” Qwen 3.5 0.8B–9B models include vision encoders, support very long context and can run locally on consumer GPUs and even iPhones (e.g., 2B at 6‑bit on iPhone 17 Pro via MLX), though early users note they can over‑reason and sometimes hallucinate on technical facts. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice), [Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-19b-arr-qwen-team))  

- **Other Chinese frontier/open models**  
  - MiniMax’s MiniMax‑M2.5, despite relatively modest size, matches or exceeds GLM‑5 and Kimi K2.5 on several benchmarks and has become a favorite among developers on OpenRouter due to a cost of ~$0.30/M tokens vs ~$5/M for Claude Opus 4.6 at similar software‑engineering scores (80.2% vs 80.8%). ([Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-19-qwen-35), [ChinAI](https://chinai.substack.com/p/chinai-348-tokens-made-in-china))

([Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-19-qwen-35))  
  - Chinese models—especially MiniMax M2.5, Moonshot’s Kimi K2.5 and GLM‑5—now account for 61% of token usage across the top‑10 models on OpenRouter, driven by 10–30× better price‑performance for agents and tooling; DeepSeek V3’s MoE architecture reportedly yields ~36× lower inference cost than GPT‑4o. ([ChinAI](https://chinai.substack.com/p/chinai-348-tokens-made-in-china))

## Infrastructure, Training & Deployment

- **Meta’s applied AI engineering division**  
  - Meta is forming a new applied AI engineering organization led by Maher Saba (formerly Reality Labs VP), reporting to CTO Andrew Bosworth, with unusually flat structure (up to ~50 reports per manager) and two teams: one for interfaces/tools and one for tasks, data collection and evaluations to build a “data engine” that accelerates model improvements alongside the Superintelligence Lab. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/meta-creates-new-applied-ai-engineering-division/))  

- **NVIDIA NIM and turnkey serving**  
  - NVIDIA’s NIM containers package models, inference engines, dependencies and OpenAI‑compatible APIs into NGC‑hosted images so developers can stand up a fully functional endpoint with a single `docker run`, eliminating custom serving code and embedding GPU‑level optimizations and quantization inside the container; NIMs can be self‑hosted on any NVIDIA GPU infra or used as managed endpoints via the NVIDIA API Catalog. ([Cobus Greyling](https://cobusgreyling.substack.com/p/nvidia-nim-makes-it-easy-for-anyone))  

- **Long‑context and memory‑efficient training**  
  - Together researchers describe a scheme combining Context Parallelism and Sequence‑parallel head chunking to cut attention memory by up to 87%, enabling training of a 5M‑context 8B model on a single 8×H100 node and highlighting that many RL post‑training setups still only exercise a fraction of nominal context due to memory constraints. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-19b-arr-qwen-team))  
  - Databricks’ FlashOptim provides AdamW/SGD/Lion variants that reduce training memory overhead from ~16 bytes/parameter to as low as 5 bytes (with gradient release), cutting an example 8B finetune’s peak from 175 GiB to 113 GiB while preserving update equivalence. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-19b-arr-qwen-team))  

- **On‑device and heterogeneous compute**  
  - A recent experiment reportedly runs a full transformer training loop on Apple’s Neural Engine via undocumented APIs, offloading heavy ops from CPU and claiming order‑of‑magnitude better energy efficiency than A100s—if replicated, this could open ANE to on‑device fine‑tuning rather than inference‑only. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  
  - Docker Desktop’s “Model Runner” now serves MLX models with an OpenAI‑compatible API on macOS, simplifying local dev on Apple Silicon, while SkyPilot’s Job Groups feature orchestrates RL workloads across heterogeneous resources (beefy GPUs for trainers, cheap GPUs for rollouts, high‑RAM CPUs for replay buffers) via a single YAML. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  
  - Taalas’s HC1 ASIC takes a “model‑as‑hardware” approach, baking weights into mask ROM so each chip runs one model but can deliver ~16–17k tokens/s per user with far lower memory traffic than GPUs. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  

- **Diffusion training speedrun**  
  - Photoroom’s PRX team demonstrates training a competitive text‑to‑image diffusion model in 24 hours on 32 H200s (~$1.5k), via pixel‑space x‑prediction (no VAE), LPIPS + DINOv2 perceptual losses, token routing with TREAD, representation alignment with REPA (DINOv3 teacher) and the Muon optimizer for 2D parameter groups, achieving solid 512→1024px quality on ~8.7M synthetic images. ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/Photoroom/prx-part3))  

- **Cloud reliability shock: AWS UAE/Bahrain**  
  - AWS reported outages in its UAE and Bahrain regions after unidentified “objects” hit a UAE data center, causing a fire and extended power loss to two clusters, disrupting banks and other customers for at least a day. ([Turing Post](https://turingpost.substack.com/p/fod142-what-is-agentic-rl-and-why))

## Agents, Tools & Governance

- **MOSAIC: safety‑first agentic RL**  
  - Microsoft researchers introduce MOSAIC, a post‑training framework that structures agent behavior as a Plan → Check → Act/Refuse loop where models explicitly emit `<safety_thoughts>` before tool calls and can invoke a refusal tool as a first‑class, terminal action. ([arXiv:2603.03205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03205))  
  - Using preference‑based RL over trajectory pairs judged by an LLM (with Group Relative Policy Optimization), MOSAIC reduces harmful behavior by up to 50%, raises refusal rates on injection attacks by >20%, cuts privacy leakage and often improves benign task performance for Qwen2.5‑7B, Qwen3‑4B‑Thinking and Phi‑4 across AgentHarm, Agent Security Bench, BFCL and PrivacyLens. ([arXiv:2603.03205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03205))  

- **Programming‑language “safety harnesses” for agents**  
  - Martin Odersky et al. propose putting tool‑using agents inside a capability‑safe language—Scala 3 with capture checking—so agents generate code rather than raw tool calls, with static types tracking capabilities and enforcing local purity and lifetimes to block secret exfiltration and unintended side effects. ([arXiv:2603.00991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00991))  
  - Their tacit prototype (an MCP server) shows LLMs can reliably generate capability‑safe code without significant performance loss while the type system prevents concrete unsafe behaviors such as writing classified data to files or leaking capabilities beyond their scope. ([arXiv:2603.00991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00991))  

- **OpenAI’s agentic governance cookbook**  
  - OpenAI’s “Agentic Governance with Bounded Autonomy” cookbook demonstrates a triage‑plus‑specialists agent architecture for a private‑equity assistant wrapped in three governance layers: per‑agent input guardrails, a central GuardrailsOpenAI pre‑flight/input/output policy wrapper and governance policies shipped as versioned pip packages. ([Cobus Greyling](https://cobusgreyling.substack.com/p/agentic-governance-with-bounded-autonomy))  
  - A tracing layer assembles a full “governance trace” (tool calls, handoffs, guardrail decisions) per query, giving enterprises an auditable safety trail while allowing agents to act autonomously within predefined bounds. ([Cobus Greyling](https://cobusgreyling.substack.com/p/agentic-governance-with-bounded-autonomy))  

- **Implicit Intelligence: testing what users don’t say**  
  - Labelbox researchers propose Implicit Intelligence and Agent‑as‑a‑World (AaW): standardized YAML scenarios with hidden rules and binary rubrics to evaluate whether agents obey unstated constraints around reasoning, catastrophic risk, privacy/security and accessibility, using Claude Opus 4.5 as the world model. ([Turing Post](https://turingpost.substack.com/p/fod142-what-is-agentic-rl-and-why))  
  - On 205 iOS‑Shortcut‑grounded scenarios (303 actions), the best model achieves 48.3% scenario pass rate (SPR) and 72.7% normalized success score (NSS), indicating large headroom in satisfying implicit constraints. ([Turing Post](https://turingpost.substack.com/p/fod142-what-is-agentic-rl-and-why))  

- **Perplexity Computer and orchestration‑as‑product**  
  - Perplexity’s new “Computer” system coordinates many specialized models and tools—routing research, coding, data work and browsing to different model families and then stitching outputs—positioning orchestration rather than owning a single frontier model as the core product. ([AI Collective](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-brief-perplexitys-router-bet))  

- **Workspace and local agents**  
  - Notion’s Custom Agents let Business and Enterprise customers define always‑on agents that run recurring workflows across Notion, Slack, calendars and more, with usage metered via new Notion credits (free on trials until May 2026) that enterprises can buy in blocks. ([AI Collective](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-brief-perplexitys-router-bet))  
  - OpenClaw, a local‑first AI agent, now has ClawHub as a one‑command marketplace for skills—popular ones include GOG (Google Workspace CLI across Gmail/Calendar/Drive/Docs/Sheets), WhatsApp CLI, Tavily search, Summarize, Obsidian (vault automation), Ontology (knowledge graphing) and n8n workflow automation. ([KDnuggets](https://www.kdnuggets.com/7-essential-openclaw-skills-you-need-right-now))  

- **Developer tooling and billing**  
  - GitNexus builds in‑browser repository knowledge graphs (via embedded KuzuDB and D3) and answers questions via Cypher graph traversal rather than plain embeddings, enabling “graph RAG” over codebases. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  
  - Stripe introduced an LLM billing proxy that lets SaaS vendors route calls to chosen models, set markups and automatically meter/token‑bill customers, foreshadowing LLM usage as a standard billing primitive. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  
  - Cursor and Notion both expanded MCP support (Cursor with interactive MCP “Apps,” Notion for meeting‑note agents), signaling continued uptake of the Model Context Protocol as an agent‑tool standard. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-19b-arr-qwen-team), [AI Collective](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-brief-perplexitys-router-bet))

## Alignment, Reward Modeling & Self‑Evolution

- **Self‑evolution vs brittle self‑play**  
  - Liu et al. argue that many self‑play LLM schemes plateau or collapse because they synthesize data without increasing “learnable information,” and formalize self‑evolution as a triadic PROPOSER–SOLVER–VERIFIER pipeline that must monotonically increase learnable information under bounded capacity (epiplexity) observers. ([arXiv:2603.02218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02218))  
  - They propose three system‑level design principles for sustained self‑evolution: asymmetric co‑evolution between SOLVER and an internal environment (PROPOSER+VERIFIER) with weak‑to‑strong and strong‑to‑weak supervision; explicit capacity growth (parameters and inference‑time compute) to match rising information; and proactive external information seeking to avoid saturation and self‑delusion. ([arXiv:2603.02218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02218))  

- **Ordinal reward modeling with Likert feedback**  
  - Afsharrad et al. develop a principled ordinal regression framework for reward modeling that treats Likert‑scale preferences (“slightly better,” “significantly better,” etc.) as ordered categories with learned thresholds, rather than bolting margins or weights onto binary Bradley‑Terry losses. ([arXiv:2603.02232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02232))  
  - They derive a negative log‑likelihood loss from cumulative link models and an all‑threshold margin loss, both jointly learning reward parameters and category thresholds, and show on multiple chat, reasoning and safety benchmarks that these ordinal methods match or beat heuristic approaches while accurately predicting preference strength (≈85% within one level). ([arXiv:2603.02232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02232))  

- **Scaling reward models without human labels**  
  - Fan et al. propose “reward‑based scaling” (RBS), training reward models entirely from web text by splitting documents into prefixes and suffixes and treating each true continuation as the preferred response vs in‑batch alternative suffixes, optimized with a Bradley‑Terry loss plus score‑centering. ([arXiv:2603.02225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02225))  
  - On 11M tokens of math‑focused web data, unsupervised RBS reward models improve RewardBench v2 accuracy by up to +7.7 points on average (including +16.1 on in‑domain math) across multiple base model families and scales, and when used for best‑of‑N selection or policy optimization, they significantly boost GSM8K and MATH accuracy, matching or exceeding supervised reward models of similar size. ([arXiv:2603.02225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02225))  

- **Agentic RL stability and CUDA optimization**  
  - ARLArena (Wang et al., highlighted in Turing Post) systematically studies design choices for policy‑gradient Agentic RL—loss aggregation, importance‑sampling clipping, advantage design and trajectory filtering—and finds sequence‑level clipping and richer advantages substantially improve stability on long‑horizon tasks, reframing agent failure as a systems/harness issue rather than “brain size.” ([Turing Post](https://turingpost.substack.com/p/fod142-what-is-agentic-rl-and-why))  
  - ByteDance’s CUDA Agent uses RL with real profiling‑based rewards to generate optimized CUDA kernels, achieving SOTA on KernelBench and large speedups over `torch.compile`, and performing competitively with frontier LLMs on hard kernels, illustrating agentic RL applied to low‑level performance. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))

## Fact‑Checking, Evaluation & Safety

- **Knowledge‑graph‑plus‑web fact‑checking agents**  
  - Gong et al. introduce WKGFC, an agentic fact‑checking framework that treats verification as an MDP: an LLM agent retrieves structured subgraphs from open KGs (e.g., Wikidata/DBpedia) via SPARQL, augments missing pieces with web search, converts web evidence into KG triples, and then reasons to decide veracity or trigger further retrieval. ([arXiv:2603.00267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00267))  
  - With prompt‑optimized policies and multi‑agent retrieval modules, WKGFC improves balanced accuracy by >5 percentage points over strong RAG and graph‑based baselines across Wikipedia (FEVER, HoVer), web (LIAR‑New, AveriTeC) and summary‑based (SummEval‑derived) datasets. ([arXiv:2603.00267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00267))  

- **AI GAMESTORE: LLMs vs humans in generated games**  
  - The AI GAMESTORE benchmark constructs 100 simple browser games by sampling popular App Store titles, using Gemini 2.5 Flash and Claude 4.5 Sonnet to synthesize p5.js versions and iteratively refining with human feedback; each game is labeled for cognitive demands such as visual processing, planning, memory and world‑model learning. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))  
  - Across ~100 human participants and models including GPT‑5.2, GPT‑5‑Mini, Gemini‑2.5‑Flash, Claude‑Opus‑4.5, Qwen‑VL‑32B and Llama‑4‑Maverick, state‑of‑the‑art LLM agents achieve <30% of human baseline scores on average and take 15–20× longer to play, underscoring large gaps in interactive, game‑like competence. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))  

- **“Bullshit” and scheming benchmarks**  
  - BullshitBench v2 adds 100 new questions across coding, medical, legal, finance and physics domains and evaluates 70+ model variants, finding that explicit “reasoning” modes often hurt performance, Anthropic models (especially Claude) dominate, and OpenAI/Google models show little improvement on this benchmark. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  
  - Neel Nanda and others caution that scheming/goal‑misgeneralization evaluations can be invalidated by contrived environments and emphasize careful environment design to get realistic signals. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  

- **Dual‑use bio uplift study**  
  - A multi‑institution study (Scale AI, SecureBio, Oxford, UC Berkeley) finds that novices with LLM access (OpenAI o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Deep Research, Claude Sonnet 3.7/Opus 4) perform 4.16× more accurately than controls on eight biosecurity‑relevant task sets, raising average accuracy from ~5% to >17% across tasks like experimental design, protocol troubleshooting and gene‑fragment reasoning. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))  
  - On a long‑form virology test, AI access doesn’t significantly change scores, but on an “agentic bio‑capabilities” benchmark involving coding and simulated lab equipment, AI‑assisted novices show strong uplift—leading the authors to warn that LLMs are materially lowering the expertise barrier for biological weapons development. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))

## Economics, Policy & Geopolitics

- **Anthropic vs Pentagon and OpenAI’s compromise**  
  - After Anthropic refused to allow its models to be used for autonomous weapons or mass domestic surveillance without contractual prohibitions, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth labeled it a “supply chain risk,” ordered federal agencies to drop Anthropic, and threatened to bar any military contractor that works with Anthropic—a move described by former White House adviser Dean Ball as “attempted corporate murder” and deemed legally dubious by Anthropic, which plans to sue. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/02/1133850/openais-compromise-with-the-pentagon-is-what-anthropic-feared/), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-nears-20-billion-revenue-run-rate-despite-pentagon-feud/))  
  - OpenAI, by contrast, signed a deal to deploy its models in classified DoW environments, embedding red lines (“no autonomous weapons, no mass domestic surveillance”) into model behavior but relying on existing laws—such as DoD’s 2023 autonomous weapons directive and the Fourth Amendment—rather than Anthropic‑style contractual prohibitions; critics note the contract excerpt bars only unlawful uses and doesn’t grant OpenAI a free‑standing veto over lawful applications. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/02/1133850/openais-compromise-with-the-pentagon-is-what-anthropic-feared/))  
  - Legal commentators warn that OpenAI’s reliance on assumptions of government lawfulness is weak given past “legalized” mass surveillance (e.g., programs later ruled unlawful), and argue that “incidental collection” loopholes may still allow broad domestic data ingestion despite contract language against “intentional” surveillance. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/02/1133850/openais-compromise-with-the-pentagon-is-what-anthropic-feared/), [Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))  

- **Anthropic’s growth and market position**  
  - Despite the Pentagon feud, Anthropic recently crossed a $19B annualized revenue run rate—more than double its ~$9B run rate at end‑2025 and up from ~$14B just weeks ago—driven heavily by enterprise adoption and its Claude Code tool, putting it close to OpenAI’s last disclosed ~$20B and contributing to an estimated $380B valuation. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-nears-20-billion-revenue-run-rate-despite-pentagon-feud/), [Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-19b-arr-qwen-team))  
  - Anthropic’s Claude app briefly became the #1 free app on the US App Store, and reported daily signups and paid subscribers both spiked after the supply‑chain‑risk announcement, indicating consumer support aligning with its stance. ([AI Collective](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-brief-perplexitys-router-bet))  

- **OpenAI capital and capacity**  
  - OpenAI is reportedly closing a ~$110B raise at a ~$730B valuation coupled with long‑term access to vast Nvidia GPU capacity, cementing its position as the best‑capitalized model lab and intensifying scrutiny of its government contracts and governance. ([Turing Post](https://turingpost.substack.com/p/fod142-what-is-agentic-rl-and-why))  

- **Chinese “tokens made in China” and hidden exports**  
  - ChinAI highlights that Chinese models’ dominance on OpenRouter effectively exports Chinese electricity and compute services: US developers’ API calls travel via undersea cables to Chinese data centers, consume Chinese power and return results in seconds, yet these “token exports” don’t appear in trade statistics, akin to earlier Bitcoin mining externalizing Chinese electricity. ([ChinAI](https://chinai.substack.com/p/chinai-348-tokens-made-in-china))  
  - The article argues that if Chinese models become default infra for global developers, switching costs could make it difficult for US policy to later restrict them—but also notes enterprises handling sensitive data largely avoid routing traffic through China‑based infra, so Chinese model penetration remains concentrated in tools and personal apps rather than core systems. ([ChinAI](https://chinai.substack.com/p/chinai-348-tokens-made-in-china))  

- **AGI economics and verification bottlenecks**  
  - “Some Simple Economics of AGI” models AGI as intersecting curves of falling “Cost to Automate” and biologically bounded “Cost to Verify,” arguing that in an agent‑rich economy the binding constraint becomes human verification bandwidth rather than intelligence, and warning of a “Hollow Economy” where agents optimize proxies, creating counterfeit utility while hidden debt accumulates. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))  
  - The authors urge heavy investment in observability and cryptographic provenance, AI‑driven “synthetic practice” to replace lost apprenticeship pathways, and robust base‑alignment so systems default to safe policies when oversight fails, framing verification infrastructure and human verifier pipelines as public goods. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))  

- **AI‑rich vs AI‑poor and adoption gaps**

## Language, World Models & Theory

- **Zipf‑preserving, long‑memory surrogates for language and DNA**  
  - Montemurro and Degli Esposti present a surrogate model that simultaneously preserves Zipf’s law (empirical symbol/word frequencies) and long‑range correlations quantified by DFA exponents by mapping fractional Gaussian noise onto the empirical histogram via a frequency‑preserving assignment. ([arXiv:2603.02213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02213))  
  - Applied to English and Latin texts and genomic DNA, the surrogates match original first‑order statistics and long‑range scaling while randomizing short‑range dependencies, offering a tool to dissect which structures in symbolic sequences arise from frequency vs long‑memory organization. ([arXiv:2603.02213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02213))  

- **Actionable world models and DeepMind Genie**  
  - The Sequence discusses DeepMind’s Genie as part of a shift from passive video generation (“screensavers”) to actionable world models that can be controlled via inputs, turning internet video into interactive, playable environments and giving agents a notion of agency and causality (e.g., understanding that actions cause objects to move). ([The Sequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-knowledge-817-deepmind))  

- **Transformer scaling theory refresh**  
  - Meta’s “Effective Theory of Wide and Deep Transformers” (resurfaced via Latent.Space) provides a 60‑page analysis of forward/backward signal propagation, hyperparameter scaling, NTK behavior and optimizer dynamics (SGD vs AdamW) in transformers, validating theoretical predictions on vision and language models and informing stable scaling rules. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))
