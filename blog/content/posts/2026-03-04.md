## Frontier and Open Models

([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-releases-gpt-5-3-instant-for-smoother-everyday-conversations-and-better-search/), [Latent.Space: AINews](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-19b-arr-qwen-team))

## Agentic Safety, Governance, and Benchmarks

- MOSAIC introduces a plan→check→act/refuse framework for agentic language models, adding explicit `<safety_thoughts>` reasoning and a first‑class refusal tool; trained via preference‑based reinforcement learning with pairwise trajectory comparisons and GRPO, it cuts harmful behavior by up to 50%, increases refusal rates on harmful/prompt‑injected tasks by >20 points, reduces privacy leakage, and often improves benign task success (e.g., lifting completion for one model from 44%→85% and another to 91%) across Qwen2.5‑7B, Qwen3‑4B‑Thinking, and Phi‑4. ([arXiv cs.CL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03205))

- “Tracking Capabilities for Safer Agents” proposes constraining agents via a capability-safe language—Scala 3 with capture‑tracking types—where capabilities (e.g., file handles, network access) are statically tracked in types to enforce local purity, control lifetimes, and prevent exfiltration; the tacit system shows LLM agents can generate capability‑safe code with little performance loss while the type system reliably blocks information leakage and unsafe side effects. ([arXiv cs.AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00991))

- MalTool demonstrates that coding LLMs (open and closed) can automatically synthesize large numbers of diverse, fully functional malicious tools (for data exfiltration, credential harvesting, compute hijacking, etc.) that execute inside agent tool ecosystems and evade both commercial malware scanners and agent-based detectors; at current GPT‑5.2 pricing, roughly $20 in API calls suffices to generate ~1,200 verified malicious tools. ([Agentic AI Weekly](https://berkeleyrdi.substack.com/p/agentic-ai-weekly-berkeley-rdi-march))

- Labelbox’s Implicit Intelligence benchmark and Agent‑as‑a‑World (AaW) framework test whether agents respect unstated constraints (e.g., catastrophic risk, privacy, accessibility) across 205 iOS‑shortcut‑grounded scenarios, with the best model achieving only 48.3% scenario pass rate (SPR) and 72.7% normalized success score (NSS), highlighting how often agents miss implicit user expectations. ([Turing Post](https://turingpost.substack.com/p/fod142-what-is-agentic-rl-and-why))

- OpenAI published an “Agentic Governance Cookbook” that implements an enterprise agent workflow where a triage agent routes to specialists (deal screening, portfolio management, investor relations) under three governance layers: per‑agent input guardrails, a centralized GuardrailsOpenAI wrapper applying pre‑flight/input/output checks, and governance policies shipped as versioned, pip‑installable Python packages enforced and traced via a Governance Trace attached to every query. ([Cobus Greyling](https://cobusgreyling.substack.com/p/agentic-governance-with-bounded-autonomy))

- ARLArena (highlighted by Turing Post) systematically studies Agentic RL training stability, finding that sequence‑level importance‑sampling clipping, more informative advantages, and trajectory filtering significantly reduce collapse on long‑horizon tasks compared to token‑level schemes, reframing agent RL as a systems problem with tunable design knobs. ([Turing Post](https://turingpost.substack.com/p/fod142-what-is-agentic-rl-and-why))

## LLM Theory, Training, and Algorithms

- “Self‑Play Only Evolves When Self‑Synthetic Pipeline Ensures Learnable Information Gain” argues that self‑evolving LLM loops often plateau because they generate synthetic data without increasing “learnable information”; the authors formalize learnable information under bounded observers using epiplexity and propose triadic roles (Proposer, Solver, Verifier) plus three design principles—asymmetric co‑evolution, capacity growth, and proactive external information seeking—to sustain self‑improvement on tasks like self‑play coding. ([arXiv cs.LG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02218))

- “Beyond Binary Preferences” introduces a principled reward‑modeling framework for Likert‑scale (ordinal) human preferences, recasting preference learning as discrete ordinal regression with learned thresholds and deriving (i) a negative log‑likelihood loss and (ii) an all‑threshold loss that subsume heuristic margin/scaling tricks; across multiple chat, reasoning, and safety benchmarks, these ordinal losses match or beat state‑of‑the‑art binary‑based approaches while more faithfully exploiting graded feedback. ([arXiv cs.LG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02232))

- “Multi‑Sourced, Multi‑Agent Evidence Retrieval for Fact‑Checking” presents WKGFC, an agentic framework that treats fact‑checking as a Markov decision process over a web‑enhanced knowledge graph: an LLM agent issues SPARQL queries to retrieve KG subgraphs, adaptively expands or supplements them with web content, converts web snippets into KG triples, and then verifies claims, with prompt‑level policy optimization via TextGrad; on Wikipedia, open‑web, and summary datasets (FEVER, HOVER, LIAR‑New, AveriTeC, SummEval), WKGFC improves balanced accuracy by >5 points over strong RAG baselines. ([arXiv cs.AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00267))

- A Physica A paper introduces a surrogate model for symbolic sequences (language, DNA) that preserves both Zipf’s law for symbol frequencies and long‑range correlations as measured by DFA: fractional Gaussian noise is mapped onto the empirical histogram via a frequency‑preserving assignment, producing surrogates that match word‑frequency distributions and scaling exponents of English/Latin texts and genomic DNA while randomizing short‑range dependencies. ([arXiv cs.CL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02213))

- Together AI details a long‑context training strategy combining Context Parallelism with sequence‑parallel head chunking to train a 5M‑token‑context 8B model on a single 8×H100 node, reducing attention memory footprint by up to 87% and enabling RL post‑training over much longer contexts than typical setups. ([Latent.Space: AINews](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-19b-arr-qwen-team))

- Databricks’ FlashOptim provides drop‑in AdamW/SGD/Lion variants that preserve update equivalence while cutting optimizer memory overhead by more than half (e.g., from ~16 bytes/parameter to 7 or even 5 bytes with gradient release), lowering peak memory for an 8B finetune from 175GiB to 113GiB. ([Latent.Space: AINews](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-19b-arr-qwen-team))

- “CUDA Agent” from ByteDance applies agentic RL to CUDA kernel synthesis using real profiling metrics (not just compilation) as rewards, achieving state‑of‑the‑art performance on KernelBench and large speedups vs `torch.compile` and standard LLM baselines, signaling a shift from “code that compiles” to “code that’s fast.” ([Latent.Space: Truth in the time of Artifice](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))

- Meta’s “Effective Theory of Wide and Deep Transformers” (resurfaced this week) provides a detailed analytical treatment of signal propagation, width scaling rules, NTK behavior, and optimizer dynamics in transformers, validated across vision and language models, and informs hyperparameter scaling at large width/depth. ([Latent.Space: Truth in the time of Artifice](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-truth-in-the-time-of-artifice))

## Biosecurity, Games, and the AGI Economy

- A multi‑lab “novice uplift” study finds that access to LLMs (OpenAI o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Deep Research, Claude Sonnet 3.7 / Opus 4) boosts non‑expert performance on dual‑use biology tasks: across eight task sets, novices with LLMs are on average 4.16× more accurate than internet‑only controls (raising accuracy from ~5%→17%), with particularly strong uplift on an “agentic bio‑capabilities” benchmark involving coding for simulated lab equipment—highlighting both LLMs’ power as universal teachers and the erosion of expertise as a barrier to bioweapons‑relevant skills. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))

- AI GAMESTORE, a benchmark of 100 LLM‑generated simplified mobile games labeled by cognitive demands (visual processing, planning, memory, world modeling, etc.), shows state‑of‑the‑art multimodal models (GPT‑5.2, Gemini‑2.5‑Pro/Flash, Claude‑Opus‑4.5, Qwen‑VL, Llama‑4‑Maverick) achieve under 30% of human baseline performance on average while taking 15–20× longer to play—demonstrating that even powerful LLMs remain weak at interactive visuomotor tasks. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))

- “Some Simple Economics of AGI” models an economy where an exponentially falling cost to automate collides with a “biologically bottlenecked” cost to verify, arguing that post‑AGI growth will be constrained not by intelligence but by scarce human verification bandwidth, with risks of a “Hollow Economy” where AI agents maximize proxy metrics while undermining real human utility; the authors call for treating verification infrastructure, human augmentation, and base‑alignment as public goods. ([Import AI](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-447-the-agi-economy-testing))

## Generative Media and Training Efficiency

- Photoroom’s PRX project reports training a competitive text‑to‑image diffusion model from scratch in 24 hours on 32 H200s (~$1,500 at $2/hr/GPU) by stacking multiple “winning tricks”: x‑prediction in pixel space (no VAE), LPIPS + DINOv2 perceptual losses applied at all noise levels, TREAD token routing that bypasses 50% of tokens through middle transformer blocks plus self‑guidance, REPA representation alignment using DINOv3 at a mid‑block, and the Muon optimizer (with Adam on non‑matrix params); training at 512px for 100k steps then fine‑tuning at 1024px for 20k on ~8.7M synthetic images yields “clearly usable” generations. ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/Photoroom/prx-part3))
