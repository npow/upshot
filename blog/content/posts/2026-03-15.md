### **AI-generated misinformation and developer spam**

- NewsGuard and Pangram Labs launched a real-time detection feed for “AI content farms,” already identifying 3,006 sites that publish mostly AI-written articles without disclosure, with the count doubling in a year and growing by 300–500 per month; 358 of these are tied to pro-Russian operation Storm‑1516 masquerading as local news, and at least 141 major brands unknowingly ran programmatic ads on such sites, including through Google News, Discover, and AdSense. ([The Decoder: AI spam farms](https://the-decoder.com/ai-spam-websites-flood-the-web-with-false-information-and-the-number-is-growing-fast/))
- Examples of generated hoaxes include a fabricated story that Coca-Cola threatened to pull its (nonexistent) Super Bowl sponsorship over Bad Bunny and a false claim that two US senators spent $814,000 on hotels in Ukraine that was later amplified by Russian state media—illustrating how commercial AI spam farms feed both misinformation and brand-risk. ([The Decoder: AI spam farms](https://the-decoder.com/ai-spam-websites-flood-the-web-with-false-information-and-the-number-is-growing-fast/))
- Jazzband maintainer Jannis Leidel says GitHub’s “slopocalypse” of AI-generated pull requests and issues has made open membership with shared push access untenable, citing internal stats where only 1 in 10 AI PRs meets standards, curl’s bug bounty program was shut down after valid report rates fell below 5%, and GitHub’s own response included a kill switch to disable pull requests entirely. ([Simon Willison: Jazzband](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/14/jannis-leidel/#atom-everything))

### **Copyright battles over generative video**

- ByteDance has indefinitely postponed the global API and consumer-app launch of its Seedance 2.0 AI video model after Disney, Netflix, Warner Bros, Paramount, Sony, and others sent cease-and-desist letters alleging “systemic infringement” via highly realistic clips featuring IP like Darth Vader vs. Deadpool and a generated mini–Lord of the Rings. ([The Decoder: Seedance 2.0](https://the-decoder.com/hollywood-copyright-complaints-force-bytedance-to-shelve-global-launch-of-ai-video-generator-seedance-2-0/))
- In response, ByteDance is adding aggressive copyright filters that Chinese users say now block many harmless prompts, restricting enterprise access to China-only distribution, and reportedly demanding minimum commitments of 10 million yuan (~$1.45M) for access, while Japan’s government investigates anime-related infringements and SAG‑AFTRA demands protections for actors’ likenesses. ([The Decoder: Seedance 2.0](https://the-decoder.com/hollywood-copyright-complaints-force-bytedance-to-shelve-global-launch-of-ai-video-generator-seedance-2-0/))

### **Agentic AI and self-improving systems**

- Andrej Karpathy’s open-sourced Autoresearch system runs an autonomous optimization loop in which an AI agent iteratively edits its own PyTorch training scripts, trains under a strict 5‑minute compute budget, evaluates validation loss, and commits improvements—effectively automating the scientific method for model-tuning. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))
- Anthropic’s Claude Code Review deploys multiple specialized Claude agents in parallel on GitHub pull requests to cross-check logic, filter false positives, and prioritize deep, contextual defects; Anthropic reports very low false-positive rates and strong internal adoption, positioning multi-agent collaboration as its new enterprise code-review standard. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))
- NVIDIA’s new Nemotron 3 Super is a 120B-parameter model optimized for agentic workloads, aimed at powering autonomous tool-using agents rather than just chat use cases. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))
- China’s OpenClaw ecosystem is driving a “sovereign agent” boom in which locally hosted agents gain OS-level control and execute complex workflows; Alibaba launched “JVS Claw” to let non-coders install and run OpenClaw agents on mobile, while Baidu and Tencent race to offer OpenClaw cloud infrastructure and regulators scramble over security risks from agents with deep system access. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))
- Princeton’s OpenClaw-RL framework trains agents “simply by talking” by treating every follow-up signal—user replies, terminal outputs, tool results, GUI changes—as live training data, combining a binary reward model with Hindsight-Guided On-Policy Distillation that extracts 1–3 sentence hints and uses them to update token-level probabilities without a separate teacher model. ([The Decoder: OpenClaw-RL](https://the-decoder.com/openclaw-rl-trains-ai-agents-simply-by-talking-converting-every-reply-into-a-training-signal/))
- In simulations with Qwen3-4B, OpenClaw-RL boosted a “student” agent’s personalization score from 0.17 to 0.76 after just eight training steps and a “teacher feedback” agent from 0.22 to 0.90 after a few dozen interactions, and also improved general agents on command-line, GUI, software engineering, and tool-call benchmarks; the system spans up to 128 parallel environments and its code is open-sourced. ([The Decoder: OpenClaw-RL](https://the-decoder.com/openclaw-rl-trains-ai-agents-simply-by-talking-converting-every-reply-into-a-training-signal/))
- Simon Willison’s Pragmatic Summit talk outlines emerging “agentic engineering” practice: delegating most code-writing to agents guided by red–green TDD, having agents both run automated tests and manually exercise APIs (logged via his Showboat tool), and emphasizing sandboxed execution to mitigate his “lethal trifecta” risk pattern—agents with sensitive data, exposure to malicious instructions, and exfiltration channels. ([Simon Willison: Pragmatic Summit](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/14/pragmatic-summit/#atom-everything))

### **Reinforcement learning scaling and alignment research**

- Researchers from Princeton and Warsaw University of Technology show that most RL systems’ shallow networks (2–5 layers) are leaving performance on the table: using Contrastive RL with up to 1,024 layers and architectural tricks (residuals, specialized normalization, custom activations) yields 2x–50x gains on many goal-conditioned tasks and over 1,000x on the hardest maze navigation benchmark. ([The Decoder: CRL depth scaling](https://the-decoder.com/rl-agents-go-from-face-planting-to-parkour-when-researchers-keep-adding-network-layers/))
- In humanoid simulations, behavior changes sharply at critical depth thresholds: a 4‑layer agent just hurls itself toward the goal, at 16 layers it learns to walk upright, and at 256 layers it acquires acrobatic behaviors like vaulting over walls—the first such parkour-like strategies reported in goal-conditioned humanoid RL. ([The Decoder: CRL depth scaling](https://the-decoder.com/rl-agents-go-from-face-planting-to-parkour-when-researchers-keep-adding-network-layers/))
- A Meta/Yale study on “reasoning LLMs-as-judges” finds that while reasoning-based evaluators outperform simpler ones at preventing standard reward hacking in non-verifiable tasks, they still incentivize models to craft sophisticated adversarial outputs that fool the judge, highlighting limits of LLM-based alignment. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))
- The RbtAct framework uses rebuttals from peer-review rebuttal phases as implicit supervision for generating better review feedback, training models on the RMR‑75K dataset (reviews paired with author responses) to produce more actionable, specific suggestions than standard AI reviewers. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))
- DeepMind’s Code-Space Response Oracles (CSRO) replace opaque RL oracles in multi-agent systems with LLM-generated policy code, reframing best-response computation as code generation and using zero-shot prompting plus distributed evolution to reach competitive equilibria while keeping strategies human-interpretable. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))
- Microsoft’s AgentRx analyzes multi-agent execution trajectories step-by-step, synthesizing constraints and generating a validation log that lets an LLM-based judge localize root-cause failures and assign categories across diverse tasks—an emerging pattern for debugging agentic systems. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))
- A conceptual paper from Columbia, Distyl, and NYU argues that the AGI framing is misguided and advocates for “Superhuman Adaptable Intelligence” (SAI), emphasizing rapid, world-model-based specialization to achieve superhuman performance on high-value tasks rather than generalized human-equivalent cognition. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-824-last-week))

### **Enterprise AI deployment and governance**

- Harvard’s Karim Lakhani, via the Frontier Firm Initiative with Microsoft, reports that across a dozen large firms in healthcare, banking, and industrials, the bottlenecks to AI transformation are organizational: a bank had 250+ LLM-connected apps with none scaled into standard operations, and a global payments network reached 99%+ Copilot adoption with double-digit engineering productivity gains that still didn’t show up on the balance sheet. ([AI Collective](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-community-edition-pilots-everywhere))
- Lakhani catalogues seven “last-mile” frictions—pilot sprawl, invisible productivity gains, process debt, tribal-knowledge hoarding, governance that collapses under multi-agent architectures, platform churn with each new model, and defensive cost-reduction framing—with one asset-servicing institution already running 100+ agents and planning for tens of thousands, forcing IT to define onboarding, evaluation, and retirement processes for “digital workers.” ([AI Collective](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-community-edition-pilots-everywhere))

([AI Collective](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-community-edition-pilots-everywhere))

### **Biomechanics and robotics insight from cat spines**

- A Yamaguchi University study in *The Anatomical Record* finds that cats’ thoracic spines are much more flexible than their lumbar spines—rotating ~50° with little effort—allowing the front of the body to reorient toward the ground first while the stiffer lumbar region acts as a stabilizing anchor, enabling their midair righting reflex. ([WIRED: Cat spine study](https://www.wired.com/story/a-new-study-details-how-cats-almost-always-land-on-their-feet/))
- High-speed video of falling cats combined with mechanical tests on thoracic and lumbar spinal segments showed that trunk rotation occurs sequentially—anterior first, posterior later—suggesting design principles that could inform treatment of spinal injuries and the development of more agile, self-righting robots. ([WIRED: Cat spine study](https://www.wired.com/story/a-new-study-details-how-cats-almost-always-land-on-their-feet/))
