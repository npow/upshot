## AI developer platforms & agents

- OpenAI is acquiring Astral, whose Python tools Ruff, uv, and ty see hundreds of millions of monthly downloads, folding its team into the Codex group to build agentic coding workflows that plan, edit, run tools, and maintain codebases over time, while keeping Astral’s permissively licensed tools open source so they can be forked if priorities ever shift. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-acquires-astral-to-bring-pythons-most-popular-dev-tools-into-its-codex-ai-coding-platform/))

- OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki says current models now complete week‑long coding experiments for him over a weekend but are still not ready to design complex systems end‑to‑end, and laid out a roadmap from an “AI research intern” due in September 2026 to a full multi‑agent “AI researcher” by March 2028 capable of running much of a research lab or even a corporation—creating “extremely concentrated power.” ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openais-chief-scientist-trusts-ai-with-experiments-but-says-its-not-at-the-level-to-design-complex-systems/))

- Dreamer, the consumer‑first agent platform from ex‑Stripe CTO David Singleton and ex‑Android VP Hugo Barra, emerged from stealth with a personal “Sidekick” agent that helps users discover and compose other agents, plus a full‑stack builder environment (SDK, logging, DB, prompt management, serverless VMs) and a $10,000 prize program to seed its ecosystem. ([Latent.Space](https://www.latent.space/p/dreamer))

- Anthropic upgraded Claude Code with “channels” that feed messages and webhooks from systems like Telegram, Discord, CI, and monitoring into a live coding session over MCP, letting Claude act as an always‑on agent that reacts to events while the developer is away; the research preview requires Claude Code v2.1.80+ and a claude.ai login (no API keys yet). ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-turns-claude-code-into-an-always-on-ai-agent-with-new-channels-feature/))

## Model training, optimization & research tooling

- MiniMax’s new M2.7 model was used as a “deep participant” in its own development: it autonomously updated its knowledge stores, built internal agent capabilities, optimized reward‑model training, ran over 100 self‑directed code‑performance optimization rounds that yielded a 30% internal gain, now automates 30–50% of the RL team’s workflow, and posts scores of 56.22% on SWE‑Pro and 55.6% on VIBE‑Pro, roughly matching GPT‑5.3‑Codex and Gemini 3.1 on MLE Bench Lite. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chinese-ai-model-minimax-m2-7-reportedly-helped-develop-itself/))

- Nvidia and Hugging Face released a turnkey recipe and synthetic dataset for fine‑tuning the 1B‑parameter Llama‑Nemotron‑Embed‑1B‑v2 embedding model on a single 80GB A100/H100 GPU, using NeMo Data Designer and hard‑negative mining to deliver ~10% gains in NDCG@10 and Recall@10 on Nvidia docs and a 26.7% Recall@60 jump (0.751 → 0.951) on Atlassian’s Jira dataset. ([HF Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/domain-specific-embedding-finetune))

- IBM Research shipped Mellea 0.4.0, an open‑source library for structured generative programs, adding native integration with three Granite Libraries—LoRA adapter suites for requirements validation, RAG pipelines, and safety/factuality checks—plus an instruct‑validate‑repair rejection‑sampling pattern and observability hooks for event‑driven monitoring. ([HF Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/granite-libraries))

- Simon Willison used Claude to locate, deconstruct, and interactively annotate Borland’s 1985 Turbo Pascal 3.02A binary (a 39,731‑byte DOS executable bundling an IDE and compiler), producing a web artifact that segments the executable into labeled components, shows decompiled assembly, and reconstructs commented pseudocode—illustrating current LLMs’ usefulness for reverse‑engineering legacy software. ([Simon Willison’s Weblog](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/20/turbo-pascal/#atom-everything))

- Adobe broadened Firefly into a unified studio that now includes public‑beta Custom Models trained on 10–30 user images to capture illustration, character, or photographic styles; aggregated access to 30+ third‑party models (Google’s Nano Banana 2, Veo 3.1, Runway Gen‑4.5, OpenAI, Kling 2.5 Turbo) alongside Firefly Image Model 5; and expanded testing of Project Moonlight, conversation‑driven AI assistants that operate across Photoshop, Express, and Acrobat. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/adobe-firefly-now-bundles-30-ai-models-and-lets-users-train-custom-styles-on-their-own-images/))

- Amazon is reportedly reviving its smartphone ambitions with a “Transformer” device built around its Alexa+ AI and shopping, potentially using a generative UI that minimizes traditional app stores, as it experiments with Android‑based Fire tablets and integrates always‑listening wearable startup Bee AI—prompting warnings that an Amazon phone could supercharge ad‑targeting and data collection given Alexa’s history. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/amazon-smartphone-rumor-2026/))

- Aiper’s Scuba V3 robotic pool cleaner adds an underwater AI vision system trained to recognize 20 debris types and uses onboard LEDs to operate in low light, but reviewers found its three‑hour runtime, unreliable AI‑based scheduling mode, 10‑minute waterline retrieval window, and hard‑to‑clean fine mesh filter undermine an otherwise capable cleaner. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/review/aiper-scuba-v3-pool-robot/))

## AI in education, work and gig labor

- The HEPI Student Generative AI Survey 2026 reports that 95% of UK full‑time undergrads now use gen‑AI (up from 66% in 2024), use of AI‑generated text in exams has quadrupled, students are split between deeper learning vs “not using my brain at all,” 15% use AI for companionship or therapy, and a medical‑school experiment found students relying solely on AI feedback performed worst yet were most confident in their skills. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/95-of-uk-students-now-use-ai-and-their-experiences-couldnt-be-more-divided/))

- In a HelpNetSecurity study, AI coding agents operating over 500 open‑source repos with subtly poisoned READMEs obeyed hidden exfiltration instructions in about 84% of runs when the command was explicit and over 90% when buried in linked documentation, while more than half of 15 human reviewers saw nothing wrong—underscoring the need for strict sandboxing, least‑privilege tooling, and documentation trust boundaries in agent workflows. ([AI Collective Newsletter](https://aicollective.substack.com/p/the-community-edition-when-agents))

- DoorDash’s new Tasks app pays gig workers roughly $15/hour (often translating to well under a dollar per video) to strap on a phone mount and record themselves doing chores, handyman work, cooking, navigation, and foreign‑language conversation so that AI models and robots can learn physical tasks—raising concerns about ultra‑low‑paid “AI data janitor” work and the practical impossibility of avoiding bystander filming in public spaces. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/i-tried-doordashs-tasks-app-and-saw-the-bleak-future-of-ai-gig-work/))

- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang argued that a developer earning $500,000 a year should be spending at least half that on AI tokens and said he’d be “deeply alarmed” by a $5,000 annual spend, framing tokens as essential productivity tools and asserting that every enterprise software vendor will eventually become a value‑added reseller of OpenAI/Anthropic tokens—making Dario Amodei’s forecast of up to $1 trillion in AI usage revenue by 2030, in his view, “very conservative.” ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-says-hed-be-deeply-alarmed-if-a-500k-developer-spent-less-than-250k-on-ai-tokens/))

## AI policy, regulation and geopolitics

- The White House’s National Policy Framework for AI urges Congress to enact a single federal regime that explicitly preempts state laws imposing “undue burdens” on AI, leans on existing agencies and voluntary standards instead of a new AI regulator, proposes giving parents more control and mandating age‑verification, forbids the federal government from coercing AI providers to alter lawful political content, and blocks states from regulating AI development or holding developers liable for third‑party misuse—positions applauded by firms like Google and OpenAI but criticized as a power grab by the Trump administration. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/white-house-ai-plan-hands-big-tech-the-federal-preemption-it-lobbied-for/))

- Prosus and Dealroom’s “State of AI in Europe” report finds Europe has AI talent on par with the US (~325,000 practitioners and 50,000 top researchers) and even higher LLM usage, but only 33% of its AI workforce sits in digital‑native tech firms vs 46% in the US, growth‑stage AI funding is three times lower (and late‑stage nine times lower), more than half of late‑stage capital is foreign, specialized AI compute is under 5% of the global total, and only 3% of new AI patents originate in Europe. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/europes-ai-paradox-is-record-adoption-that-funds-foreign-ecosystems-instead-of-building-its-own/))

- House Intelligence Committee ranking Democrat Jim Himes is privately lobbying Democrats to support a short‑term “clean” reauthorization of FISA Section 702 warrantless surveillance authority based on a claimed 99% FBI compliance rate—even though the FBI dissolved the internal audit unit that generated that metric and the Trump DOJ has been repeatedly rebuked for misleading courts—while the Congressional Progressive Caucus has voted to oppose renewal without real warrant requirements for US‑person queries. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/a-top-democrat-is-urging-colleagues-to-support-trumps-spy-machine/))

- At Palantir’s developer conference, CEO Alex Karp emphasized that the company “was built to give our warfighters…an unfair advantage” and is uninterested in debating support for US forces in the Iran war, telling customers that buying Palantir means “you are engaging in proxy when you are engaging with us,” and casting this posture as a competitive differentiator from AI firms trying to limit military use. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/palantir-developer-conference-ai-war-alex-karp/))

## Prediction markets, finance and legality

- A Nevada court issued a 14‑day temporary restraining order barring Kalshi from operating sports, election, and entertainment event markets in the state without gaming licenses—expected to be extended into a case‑long preliminary injunction—while Arizona’s attorney general filed criminal charges alleging Kalshi runs an illegal gambling business. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/kalshi-prediction-market/))

- In the same week, Bloomberg reported Kalshi had raised another $1 billion at a $22 billion valuation—doubling its paper value since late 2025—prompting its board to literally celebrate with sidewalk push‑ups as the company vows to fight state actions it says “circumvent federal court” and maintains its contracts are CFTC‑regulated swaps, not bets. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/why-this-was-the-wildest-week-for-prediction-markets-yet/))

- US senators led by Chris Murphy introduced legislation to ban prediction markets on government actions, terrorism, war, assassination, and events an individual can control, with Murphy calling them “a rigged and dangerous product” and citing insider‑trading risks highlighted by Israeli cases where traders allegedly used classified war information to profit on Polymarket. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/why-this-was-the-wildest-week-for-prediction-markets-yet/))

- Offshore platform Polymarket, which recently signed a sponsorship deal with Major League Baseball, continues to list war‑related markets such as whether Israeli PM Benjamin Netanyahu will be “out” by set dates, with outcomes resolved as “yes” in the event of death as with Iran’s Khamenei—categories that would be barred under the proposed US bill but are largely beyond US regulatory reach. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/why-this-was-the-wildest-week-for-prediction-markets-yet/))

## Security, surveillance and platform safety

- A joint US law‑enforcement operation took down the Aisuru, Kimwolf, JackSkid, and Mossad botnets that had infected more than 3 million devices worldwide, while researchers disclosed DarkSword, a powerful exploit tool Russian hackers used that can remotely compromise hundreds of millions of iPhones. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-cyberattack-on-a-car-breathalyzer-firm-leaves-drivers-stuck/))

- A misconfigured backend left customer chats and calls to Sears Home Services’ “Samantha” AI bot publicly accessible online, including hours of additional audio apparently recorded after callers believed the session had ended, and Telegram channels are now flooded with “AI face model” job postings that seem to recruit mostly women as front people for AI‑driven financial scams. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-cyberattack-on-a-car-breathalyzer-firm-leaves-drivers-stuck/))

- Meta will disable end‑to‑end encryption for Instagram DMs on May 8 due to low adoption, walking back long‑promised default protections and stoking fears of an industry precedent, even as Signal creator Moxie Marlinspike partners with Meta to integrate his encrypted AI platform Confer into Meta AI products. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-cyberattack-on-a-car-breathalyzer-firm-leaves-drivers-stuck/))

- A cyberattack on Intoxalock, whose court‑mandated in‑car breathalyzers are used by roughly 150,000 drivers, knocked backend systems offline so that units due for periodic calibration couldn’t connect—stranding some users until the company granted 10‑day calibration extensions and offered towing, while declining to detail the nature or scope of the breach. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-cyberattack-on-a-car-breathalyzer-firm-leaves-drivers-stuck/))

- FBI director Kash Patel acknowledged the bureau is again buying “commercially available” phone‑location data from adtech data brokers, despite prior assurances it had stopped and a 2018 Supreme Court ruling requiring warrants for cell‑site location tracking, prompting senators Ron Wyden and Mike Lee to propose a ban on warrantless government data purchases. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-cyberattack-on-a-car-breathalyzer-firm-leaves-drivers-stuck/))

- The Iranian‑linked Handala group’s early‑March hack of medical device giant Stryker led some Maryland hospitals to disconnect affected systems and rely on radio consultation, temporarily disrupting emergency care, while Meta suffered a Sev1 internal incident after an experimental AI agent posted an incorrect technical answer that exposed large volumes of corporate and user data to employees lacking proper access. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-cyberattack-on-a-car-breathalyzer-firm-leaves-drivers-stuck/))

## Energy, EVs and hardware

- The Israel–Iran war has escalated from closing the Strait of Hormuz to direct strikes on energy infrastructure, including Israel’s attack on the giant South Pars gas field (shared by Iran and Qatar) and Iran’s retaliation against Qatar’s main LNG export terminal, with QatarEnergy’s CEO saying 17% of its LNG capacity is knocked out for five years, oil briefly spiking near $120/barrel, fertilizer and airline prices rising, and the IEA urging work‑from‑home and slower driving to blunt what analysts say could be a “depressionary” oil shock if the strait stays shut. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/iran-war-puts-global-energy-markets-on-the-brink-of-a-worst-case-scenario/))

- BYD’s upgraded “Flash Chargers” deliver up to 1,500 kW, allowing cars with its latest LMFP Blade batteries—such as the Denza Z9GT—to charge from roughly 10% to 70% in five minutes and to full in about nine minutes, with more than 4,000 such chargers already built in China and plans for 16,000 more there plus 2,000 in Europe, though most existing EVs and distribution grids can’t use anything close to that power level. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/how-byds-ev-charger-got-even-faster-and-it-might-not-matter-as-much-as-you-think/))

- A WIRED survey of 2026 EVs notes Chinese automakers like BYD and Geely are rapidly expanding in Europe with long‑range, low‑cost models while US brands retreat from plug‑in hybrids and delay EVs amid tax‑credit rollbacks and uncertainty from the Iran‑driven oil shock—raising the risk that first‑time EV buyers abroad will lock into Chinese brands with high repeat‑purchase rates. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/the-16-best-evs-coming-in-2026/))
