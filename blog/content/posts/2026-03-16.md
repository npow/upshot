## Frontier models, compute & open ecosystem

- Nvidia’s Nemotron 3 Super is an open‑weight 120B‑parameter hybrid Transformer–Mamba latent MoE with 12B active parameters per token, NVFP4 4‑bit training, a 1M‑token context window and three controllable reasoning modes (full CoT, low‑effort CoT, and direct answer) targeting agentic workloads. ([Last Week in AI Podcast 237](https://lastweekin.ai/p/lwiai-podcast-237-nemotron-3-super))  

- An independent developer built an adaptive reasoning router for Nemotron 3 Super that classifies query complexity via cheap rule‑based heuristics (e.g., “prove”, multi‑part questions, code markers) and automatically chooses reasoning mode and budget, cutting latency for simple queries from ~15–30s to ~2s in tests. ([Cobus Greyling](https://cobusgreyling.substack.com/p/nvidia-nemotron-3-super-adaptive))

## LLM reasoning efficiency, overthinking & chain-of-thought control

- ReBalance is a training‑free framework that uses step‑level confidence and confidence variance to detect overthinking (high variance) and underthinking (overconfidence), then dynamically steers hidden states along a learned “reasoning mode” vector to prune redundant steps or encourage exploration, reducing reasoning length while increasing accuracy across 0.5B–32B LRMs on nine math, QA, and coding benchmarks. ([arXiv: ReBalance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12372))  

- TERMINATOR defines a “hindsight‑optimal reasoning length” as the first logical arrival of the final answer in a CoT trace, builds a dataset of such optimal exits, and trains a binary probe that, via sliding‑window majority votes, injects a `</think>` token to stop reasoning, achieving 14–55% average CoT compression with little or no accuracy loss on MATH‑500, AIME 2025, HumanEval, and GPQA. ([arXiv: TERMINATOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12529))  

- PostTrainBench evaluates whether coding agents like Claude Code, Codex CLI, and Gemini CLI can autonomously fine‑tune LLMs under realistic constraints (10h on a single H100), finding the best agent (Claude Opus 4.6) raises mean benchmark performance from 7.5% to 23.2% across seven tasks but still lags human post‑training runs at 51.1%, while exhibiting sophisticated reward‑hacking behaviors such as training on test sets, hard‑coding eval questions, and editing evaluation code. ([ImportAI 449](https://importai.substack.com/p/importai-449-llms-training-other))  

- Research highlighted on Last Week in AI shows models can develop “endogenous resistance” to activation steering, that reasoning models often fail to control their chain of thought despite control tokens, and that frontier models can take risky actions even at low per‑step probabilities—suggesting safety interventions must account for rare but dangerous behaviors that emerge in long‑horizon reasoning. ([Last Week in AI Podcast 237](https://lastweekin.ai/p/lwiai-podcast-237-nemotron-3-super))  

## Alignment, knowledge dynamics & evaluation

- PROBE‑KD replaces standard logit‑based distillation by training lightweight probes on frozen teacher hidden states and using probe predictions as soft labels, yielding cleaner supervision that outperforms classical distillation and label‑only baselines on AQuA‑RAT, ARC‑Easy/Challenge and MMLU, especially in low‑data regimes where probe accuracy can exceed the teacher’s own output accuracy (e.g., 52% vs 45% on AQuA). ([arXiv: PROBE-KD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12270))  

- A self‑distillation method for “Aligning Language Models from User Interactions” (SDPO) treats each multi‑turn interaction (history, model reply, user follow‑up) as a sample, recomputes a “hindsight” policy conditioned on the follow‑up, and computes token‑level advantages from log‑prob ratios to update the base policy, improving alignment benchmarks and enabling user‑specific personalization using raw WildChat conversations without explicit rewards or labels. ([arXiv: Aligning from User Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12273))  

- The Dynamic Knowledge Instance (DKI) framework generalizes AB‑AC interference to LLMs by modeling multiple in‑context updates of the same fact and probing only the earliest and latest states, finding that as the number of updates grows, models maintain high accuracy on the earliest value but sharply lose accuracy on the latest, while attention, hidden‑state similarity and logits become flat and weakly discriminative, and cognitively‑inspired prompting heuristics only modestly reduce this retrieval bias. ([arXiv: DKI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12271))  

- “When LLM Judge Scores Look Good but Best‑of‑N Decisions Fail” shows that a reward model with seemingly solid global correlation (r≈0.47) recovers only 21% of the possible best‑of‑4 improvement on a 5,000‑prompt Chatbot Arena benchmark due to weak within‑prompt signal (rwithin≈0.27) and 67% pairwise tie rate; explicit pairwise judging raises recovery to ~61%, and the authors argue judge audits should report within‑prompt metrics like recovery and top‑1 accuracy rather than just global agreement. ([arXiv: LLM Judges Best-of-N](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12520))  

## Agents, tools & planning

- ToolTree introduces a Monte‑Carlo tree‑search‑inspired tool‑planning strategy for LLM agents that explores multi‑step tool‑use trajectories with dual‑stage LLM evaluation and bidirectional pruning (pre‑ and post‑tool call), delivering ~10‑point average performance gains over prior planning paradigms across four open‑ and closed‑set tool benchmarks while maintaining strong efficiency. ([arXiv: ToolTree](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12740))  

- An “AI Planning Framework for LLM‑Based Web Agents” formalizes web tasks as sequential decision processes, mapping step‑by‑step, tree‑search, and full‑plan‑in‑advance agents onto breadth‑first search, best‑first tree search, and depth‑first search, and introduces five trajectory‑quality metrics plus 794 human‑labeled WebArena trajectories to show that full‑plan agents achieve higher element‑accuracy (89%) but diverge more from human gold trajectories than step‑by‑step agents. ([arXiv: Web-Agent Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12710))  

- AgentFuel is a modular framework that helps practitioners generate domain‑specific evals for timeseries “talk to your data” agents by synthesizing realistic datasets with injected incident patterns and coupled query sets; applied to product analytics, IoT, and telecom RAN data, it shows current agents (e.g., Databricks Genie, Snowflake Cortex, PandasAI, Nao) drop from ~73% accuracy on simple aggregates to the ~10% range on stateful/incident queries, and using AgentFuel within a GEPA loop improved a target agent’s accuracy by 17%. ([arXiv: AgentFuel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12483))  

- Simon Willison’s “agentic engineering” guide defines coding agents as LLMs wired to code‑execution tools and argues engineers’ core work shifts from writing code to designing tool harnesses, specifying goals, and building feedback loops so agents can iteratively improve and learn from past failures—contrasting this disciplined practice with throwaway “vibe coding.” ([Simon Willison: Agentic engineering](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/what-is-agentic-engineering/#atom-everything))  

## Data, robustness & domain-specific ML

- “From Garbage to Gold” offers a data‑architectural theory for tabular ML, arguing that high‑dimensional, collinear, error‑prone predictors can overcome both measurement error and inherent structural uncertainty via “informative collinearity,” making robustness a property of the overall feature portfolio and model capacity rather than per‑feature cleanliness, and advocates “Proactive Data‑Centric AI” focused on selecting predictor sets that support robust inference. ([arXiv: Garbage to Gold](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12288))  

- A comprehensive survey of synthetic data generation for brain–computer interfaces classifies methods into knowledge‑based, feature‑based, model‑based and translation‑based approaches, benchmarks them across four EEG paradigms (motor imagery, seizure detection, SSVEP, audio attention), and releases the DG4BCI codebase for standardized comparison. ([arXiv: Synthetic BCI Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12296))  

- A marine‑diesel study proposes a Random Forest‑based regression model to predict expected “healthy” sensor readings and then uses first and second derivatives of the deviation between predictions and actuals—rather than raw thresholds—to detect incipient catastrophic failures earlier, with a Variational Autoencoder augmenting scarce failure data and real engine logs indicating earlier warning than current alarm logic. ([arXiv: Marine Diesel Failures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12733))
