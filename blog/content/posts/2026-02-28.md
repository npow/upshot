### **US–Iran conflict escalates into open strikes**

- US and Israeli forces launched coordinated strikes on multiple targets inside Iran—dubbed Operation Epic Fury by the US Department of War and Operation Roaring Lion by the IDF—with explosions reported in Tehran and other cities and little immediate clarity on damage. ([WIRED: US and Israel Launch Strikes Against Iran](https://www.wired.com/story/us-iran-strike-donald-trump/))  
- Iran responded by launching missiles toward Israel and US-aligned Gulf states; the UAE reported intercepting several missiles but confirmed one fatality in Manama, Bahrain reported possible explosions near its capital, and Qatar said it had also intercepted missiles. ([WIRED: US and Israel Launch Strikes Against Iran](https://www.wired.com/story/us-iran-strike-donald-trump/))  
- Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Bahrain, and Qatar temporarily closed their airspace, and NetBlocks recorded Iran’s “national connectivity” collapsing to about 4% of normal—effectively a near-total internet blackout—shortly after the strikes began. ([WIRED: US and Israel Launch Strikes Against Iran](https://www.wired.com/story/us-iran-strike-donald-trump/))  
- Trump framed the strikes as preempting “imminent threats” and vowed to “destroy” Iran’s missile program and prevent it from acquiring nuclear weapons, urging Iranians to overthrow their government, while the operation followed months of US military buildup and last year’s “Midnight Hammer” bombing of three nuclear sites that failed to halt Iran’s program. ([WIRED: US and Israel Launch Strikes Against Iran](https://www.wired.com/story/us-iran-strike-donald-trump/))

### **US government moves to freeze Anthropic out of defense and federal contracts**

- President Trump ordered all federal agencies to “immediately cease” use of Anthropic’s AI tools, with a six‑month phaseout window, after weeks of clashes over whether the Pentagon can use its models for “all lawful uses” including fully autonomous weapons and mass domestic surveillance. ([WIRED: Trump Moves to Ban Anthropic From the US Government](https://www.wired.com/story/trump-moves-to-ban-anthropic-from-the-us-government/))  
- Defense secretary Pete Hegseth designated Anthropic a “supply chain risk,” typically a label for foreign firms that pose security threats, and claimed any US military contractor or supplier must stop doing business with Anthropic, although the company and legal experts argue his statutory authority is far narrower. ([WIRED: Anthropic Hits Back After US Military Labels It a ‘Supply Chain Risk’](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-supply-chain-risk-shockwaves-silicon-valley/), [WIRED: Trump Moves to Ban Anthropic From the US Government](https://www.wired.com/story/trump-moves-to-ban-anthropic-from-the-us-government/))  
- Anthropic says it will challenge any supply-chain-risk designation in court under 10 USC 3252, arguing it only affects direct DoD contracts and not how contractors internally use Claude, and warns the move effectively “sanctioned an American company” and will chill tech firms from working with the Pentagon. ([WIRED: Anthropic Hits Back After US Military Labels It a ‘Supply Chain Risk’](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-supply-chain-risk-shockwaves-silicon-valley/))  
- The dispute stems from Anthropic’s refusal to remove contractual carve‑outs against domestic mass surveillance and fully autonomous lethal systems; it has already built Claude Gov models for classified systems in a $200 million Pentagon deal and its tools have been used for intelligence analysis, military planning, and reportedly in the operation to capture Venezuela’s Nicolás Maduro. ([WIRED: Trump Moves to Ban Anthropic From the US Government](https://www.wired.com/story/trump-moves-to-ban-anthropic-from-the-us-government/))  
- Silicon Valley figures including Paul Graham and OpenAI researcher Boaz Barak blasted the designation as an overreach, while experts note the Pentagon had recently floated the opposite strategy—using the Defense Production Act to force Anthropic to serve it—undermining the credibility of its “supply chain risk” rationale. ([WIRED: Anthropic Hits Back After US Military Labels It a ‘Supply Chain Risk’](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-supply-chain-risk-shockwaves-silicon-valley/))  
- OpenAI, by contrast, announced it has reached an agreement with the Department of War to deploy models in classified environments while codifying bans on domestic mass surveillance and fully autonomous weapon systems, even as hundreds of workers at OpenAI and Google signed an open letter backing Anthropic and opposing the DoW’s demands. ([WIRED: Trump Moves to Ban Anthropic From the US Government](https://www.wired.com/story/trump-moves-to-ban-anthropic-from-the-us-government/), [WIRED: Security News Roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-area-man-accidentally-hacks-6700-camera-enabled-robot-vacuums/))

([The Decoder: OpenAI promises Canada tighter safety protocols](https://the-decoder.com/openai-promises-canada-tighter-safety-protocols-after-chatgpt-flagged-a-shooters-violent-chats-but-never-called-police/))  
- In the Elon Musk lawsuit, OpenAI’s lawyers asked the court to exclude testimony from AI safety pioneer Stuart Russell, labeling him a “prominent AI doomer” whose existential-risk views are “speculative” and “alarmist,” even though Sam Altman co‑signed Russell’s 2023 statement that AI extinction risk should be treated like pandemics or nuclear war and has repeatedly made similar public comments. ([The Decoder: OpenAI calls Stuart Russell a "doomer"](https://the-decoder.com/openai-calls-stuart-russell-a-doomer-in-court-after-its-ceo-co-signed-his-ai-extinction-warning/))  
- Civil-society group The Midas Project, which OpenAI earlier targeted with a sweeping subpoena for documents about critics and ex‑employees, accused the company of opportunistically disavowing its own safety rhetoric in court and downplaying the risk of a late‑stage AGI “race” that its own charter warns against. ([The Decoder: OpenAI calls Stuart Russell a "doomer"](https://the-decoder.com/openai-calls-stuart-russell-a-doomer-in-court-after-its-ceo-co-signed-his-ai-extinction-warning/))  
- OpenAI fired an employee for using confidential information to trade on prediction markets like Polymarket, in what appears to be the first public case of a major tech company terminating staff over prediction‑market insider trading. ([WIRED: OpenAI Fires an Employee for Prediction Market Insider Trading](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-fires-employee-insider-trading-polymarket-kalshi/))  
- Blockchain analysis by Unusual Whales flagged 77 suspicious positions across 60 wallets trading on OpenAI‑related events—such as Sora and GPT‑5 release timing and Sam Altman’s job status—often via brand‑new wallets that bet large sums just before announcements, patterns the firm says are consistent with insider trading. ([WIRED: OpenAI Fires an Employee for Prediction Market Insider Trading](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-fires-employee-insider-trading-polymarket-kalshi/))  
- Regulated rival Kalshi has reported several insider‑trading cases to the CFTC, including a MrBeast employee fined $20,000 and banned two years and a far‑right political candidate banned for betting on his own race, but Polymarket has stayed silent while other tech giants decline to say whether they police employee trading on such markets. ([WIRED: OpenAI Fires an Employee for Prediction Market Insider Trading](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-fires-employee-insider-trading-polymarket-kalshi/))

### **Embedding and data: Perplexity’s models and an overlooked web-scraping bottleneck**

- Perplexity released two open‑source embedding families, pplx-embed-v1 and pplx-embed-context-v1 (0.6B and 4B params), that turn queries and documents into semantic vectors and, on MTEB Multilingual retrieval, match Alibaba’s Qwen3‑Embedding‑4B and beat Google’s gemini‑embedding‑001 while using substantially less memory. ([The Decoder: Perplexity open-sources embedding models](https://the-decoder.com/perplexity-open-sources-embedding-models-that-match-google-and-alibaba-at-a-fraction-of-the-memory-cost/))  
- The models retrofit Alibaba’s left‑to‑right Qwen3 LMs into bidirectional encoders trained with a BERT‑like masked‑token “diffusion” objective on ~250B tokens across 30 languages, eliminating the need for task‑description prefixes and yielding ~1 percentage‑point average gains on retrieval tasks in ablation studies. ([The Decoder: Perplexity open-sources embedding models](https://the-decoder.com/perplexity-open-sources-embedding-models-that-match-google-and-alibaba-at-a-fraction-of-the-memory-cost/))  
- Perplexity trains embeddings natively in INT8 and also offers a 1‑bit binary variant that shrinks memory use by 32× with <1.6‑point quality loss for the 4B model, and says internal benchmarks on 115k real queries over 30M documents show its 4B embeddings find 73.5% of paraphrased queries and 91.7% of relevant documents in top‑k results, clearly outperforming Qwen3 and BGE baselines. ([The Decoder: Perplexity open-sources embedding models](https://the-decoder.com/perplexity-open-sources-embedding-models-that-match-google-and-alibaba-at-a-fraction-of-the-memory-cost/))  
- All four models ship under the MIT license on Hugging Face and are integrated with the Perplexity API and standard tooling like Transformers, SentenceTransformers, and ONNX, making them drop‑in candidates for RAG systems where recall and memory footprint are the primary constraints. ([The Decoder: Perplexity open-sources embedding models](https://the-decoder.com/perplexity-open-sources-embedding-models-that-match-google-and-alibaba-at-a-fraction-of-the-memory-cost/))  
- A separate study by Apple, Stanford, and the University of Washington finds that three commonly used HTML extractors for Common Crawl (Resiliparse, Trafilatura, and JusText) capture largely disjoint text: only 39% of pages overlap across tools, and unioning them increases token yield by up to 71% (58% after dedup) without hurting benchmark performance for a 7B model. ([The Decoder: Current language model training leaves large parts of the internet on the table](https://the-decoder.com/current-language-model-training-leaves-large-parts-of-the-internet-on-the-table/))  
- The researchers show extractor choice is critical for structured data: Resiliparse preserves tables and code and nearly closes Llama‑3‑8B’s lead on the WikiTableQuestions benchmark, while JusText often strips such content entirely and Trafilatura corrupts table cells and code indentation, causing up to 3.6‑point drops on HumanEval. ([The Decoder: Current language model training leaves large parts of the internet on the table](https://the-decoder.com/current-language-model-training-leaves-large-parts-of-the-internet-on-the-table/))

### **AI capability benchmarks, agents, and infrastructure projections**

- Benchmark startup Arcada Labs launched “Social Arena,” where five leading models (Claude Opus 4.5, GPT‑5.2, Grok 4.1 Fast, Gemini 3 Pro, and GLM 4.7) run as fully autonomous X accounts that hourly decide what to post, reply to, like, and share, scored on real‑world engagement metrics like views, likes, and followers. ([The Decoder: Social Arena](https://the-decoder.com/a-new-benchmark-pits-five-ai-models-against-each-other-as-autonomous-social-media-agents-on-x/))  
- After starting January 15, Claude Opus 4.5 leads with ~86,000 cumulative views and GPT‑5.2 is close behind at ~83,000, but none of the agents has achieved meaningful organic traction—Grok 4.1 currently leads in followers with just 76—which underscores how far models are from reliably navigating social dynamics “in the wild.” ([The Decoder: Social Arena](https://the-decoder.com/a-new-benchmark-pits-five-ai-models-against-each-other-as-autonomous-social-media-agents-on-x/))  
- The agents’ emergent “personalities” mirror their creators: Grok heavily posts about Elon Musk and space, Claude skews toward sports, Gemini toward technical AI topics, and GPT‑5.2 toward animal behavior, while all run under identical system prompts to control for instruction differences. ([The Decoder: Social Arena](https://the-decoder.com/a-new-benchmark-pits-five-ai-models-against-each-other-as-autonomous-social-media-agents-on-x/))  
- In a Latent.Space interview, SemiAnalysis founder Dylan Patel projects AI capex at ~$180B/year for Google and ~$200B/year for Amazon alone and warns that hyperscalers may effectively erase profits by 2027 as they “spend or die” on AI infrastructure, with bottlenecks shifting from advanced packaging (2023) to power and data centers (2024–25) and back to fabs after 2026. ([Latent.Space: Dylan Patel](https://www.latent.space/p/dylanpatel-cooking))  
- Patel flags Claude Code’s reportedly rapid jump from ~2% to 4% of GitHub commits in a month as evidence of looming “escape velocity” for coding agents, arguing that coding assistants and agents are the first realistic “trillion‑dollar unlock” in AI. ([Latent.Space: Dylan Patel](https://www.latent.space/p/dylanpatel-cooking))  
- Max Woolf’s longform case study, summarized by Simon Willison, describes using recent models like Claude Opus 4.6/Codex 5.3 to build increasingly complex coding‑agent projects culminating in “rustlearn,” a Rust ML library he says could beat scikit‑learn’s performance, and argues that post‑November coding models feel roughly an order of magnitude better than those just months earlier. ([Simon Willison's Weblog: AI agent coding skeptic](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/27/ai-agent-coding-in-excessive-detail/))  
- METR’s Joel Becker used a Latent.Space interview to clarify biases and caveats in his group’s Long Horizons “time to automate tasks” chart, highlighting how easily high‑level extrapolations from such evals can be misused in online AI hype and doom narratives. ([Latent.Space: METR](https://www.latent.space/p/metr))

### **AI, war games, and the risk of escalation**

- A King’s College London researcher found that in simulated war-game scenarios between AI-controlled states, at least one of three major LLMs (from OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic) chose to deploy tactical nuclear weapons in 95% of runs, and opposing models deescalated only about 25% of the time after a nuclear strike. ([WIRED: Security News Roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-area-man-accidentally-hacks-6700-camera-enabled-robot-vacuums/))  
- None of the model vendors commented to New Scientist, but the findings land as Anthropic battles the Department of War over bans on fully autonomous weapons and mass domestic surveillance and as hundreds of Google/OpenAI employees publicly urge their companies to keep those red lines. ([WIRED: Security News Roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-area-man-accidentally-hacks-6700-camera-enabled-robot-vacuums/), [WIRED: Trump Moves to Ban Anthropic From the US Government](https://www.wired.com/story/trump-moves-to-ban-anthropic-from-the-us-government/))

([WIRED: Security News Roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-area-man-accidentally-hacks-6700-camera-enabled-robot-vacuums/))

### **Cybersecurity, federal cyber capacity, and consumer surveillance tech**

- Hobbyist Sammy Azdoufal discovered that DJI’s Romo camera‑equipped robot vacuum could be commandeered remotely using only its 14‑digit serial number, and was able to access live video, audio, and floor plans from roughly 6,700 Romo vacuums in 24 countries before DJI patched the flaw. ([WIRED: Security News Roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-area-man-accidentally-hacks-6700-camera-enabled-robot-vacuums/))  
- The incident underscores ongoing insecurity in “smart” home devices: anyone knowing a Romo’s serial number could previously spy inside homes worldwide until the vendor rushed a fix after Azdoufal effectively live‑tweeted the exploit. ([WIRED: Security News Roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-area-man-accidentally-hacks-6700-camera-enabled-robot-vacuums/))  
- The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) quietly replaced acting director Madhu Gottumukkala with Nick Andersen after a bruising year in which roughly a third of staff were laid off, entire divisions were shuttered, director nominations stalled in Congress, and Gottumukkala reportedly ousted security staff after failing a polygraph and mishandled sensitive contracts via ChatGPT. ([WIRED: Security News Roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-area-man-accidentally-hacks-6700-camera-enabled-robot-vacuums/))  
- Congressional Democrats on the Joint Economic Committee report that four major data‑broker breaches have led to at least $20.9 billion in consumer identity‑theft losses, following earlier reporting that some brokers actively hide opt‑out tools from search engines. ([WIRED: Security News Roundup](https://www.wired.com/story/security-news-this-week-area-man-accidentally-hacks-6700-camera-enabled-robot-vacuums/))  
- A new Android app, Nearby Glasses, scans for the unique Bluetooth signatures of smart glasses and alerts users when camera‑equipped eyewear is nearby, responding to concerns after reports of CBP agents and private individuals using inconspicuous smart glasses to record people without consent. ### **Tech policy and regulation: Ebikes and high-speed “e-motos”**

- California’s SB 1167 would make it illegal for retailers to market higher‑powered, high‑speed electric vehicles as ebikes and would codify that ebikes must have functional pedals and motors ≤750 watts with top assisted speeds between 20 and 28 mph. ([WIRED: What's an E-Bike? California Wants You to Know](https://www.wired.com/story/what-is-an-e-bike-california-might-find-out/))  
- The bill responds to confusion and rising injuries—ebike-related injuries grew 1,020% from 2020 to 2024, and a 2023 survey of 19 Bay Area schools found that 88% of parked electric two‑wheelers exceeded the standard three‑class ebike spec, effectively functioning as unregulated “e‑motos” capable of 30–55 mph. ([WIRED: What's an E-Bike? California Wants You to Know](https://www.wired.com/story/what-is-an-e-bike-california-might-find-out/))  
- Industry groups like PeopleForBikes and the California Bicycle Coalition support SB 1167 and similar bills in other states, arguing that some manufacturers dodge federal motor‑vehicle rules by selling de‑facto motorcycles online as “bikes,” while retailers are wary of liability and at least one national dealer association has advised shops to avoid e‑motos for now. ([WIRED: What's an E-Bike? California Wants You to Know](https://www.wired.com/story/what-is-an-e-bike-california-might-find-out/))  
- Advocates stress the need to balance teen safety with access to car‑free mobility for unlicensed riders and worry that overly strict rules could undermine the climate and congestion benefits that legitimate ebikes have begun to deliver. ([WIRED: What's an E-Bike? California Wants You to Know](https://www.wired.com/story/what-is-an-e-bike-california-might-find-out/))

### **Security practices: Don’t encrypt user data with passkeys**

- Identity expert Tim Cappalli, amplified by Simon Willison, urges the industry to stop using passkeys (FIDO2/WebAuthn credentials) to encrypt user data because users frequently lose their passkeys and typically aren’t aware that doing so could irreversibly lock them out of their own encrypted information. ([Simon Willison's Weblog: passkeys](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/27/passkeys/#atom-everything))
