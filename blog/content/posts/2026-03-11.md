- The AgentOS concept paper argues for replacing GUI-centric desktops with a “Single Port” natural-language/voice interface backed by an Agent Kernel that parses intent, orchestrates multiple agents via MCP-like protocols, schedules LLM resources, and treats applications as composable “Skills-as-Modules,” reframing the OS as a continuous knowledge-discovery and data-mining pipeline. ([AgentOS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08938))

## Multi-Agent Protocols and Governance

- The LLM Delegate Protocol (LDP) proposes an AI-native communication layer for multi-agent systems that exposes model identity cards (family, size, reasoning profile, latency/cost hints), supports negotiated payload modes from plain text to structured semantic frames and embeddings, maintains governed multi-round sessions with stateful context, tracks structured provenance, and enforces trust domains. ([LDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08852))

- In experiments using local Ollama models and Gemini as a judge, LDP’s identity-aware routing cut latency ~12× on easy tasks by sending them to cheaper/faster models, semantic-frame payloads reduced token counts by 37% without quality loss, governed sessions eliminated 39% token overhead at 10 turns versus stateless calls, and simulations suggested large gains in attack detection (96% vs 6%) and failure recovery (100% vs 35%), though naive confidence metadata harmed synthesis quality. ([LDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08852))

## LLM Agents and Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Tasks

- The Guardian LLM Pipeline for missing-child investigations orchestrates multiple specialized LLMs for summarization, schema-aligned extraction, and weak labeling behind a centralized consensus engine that normalizes outputs, scores cross-model agreement at the field level, repairs malformed JSON via constrained prompts, and exposes detailed traces for auditability, with models QLoRA-fine-tuned on curated case datasets. ([Guardian LLM Pipeline](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08954))

- Guardian’s three-layer predictive stack combines (1) a sparse Markov chain over a geospatial grid with road-accessibility costs, seclusion preferences, corridor bias, and separate day/night transition parameters; (2) a reinforcement-learning agent that converts risk surfaces into compact, high-value search zones; and (3) an LLM-based quality-assurance layer that post-hoc validates RL-generated plans, producing interpretable 24/48/72‑hour search products in a synthetic case study. ([Guardian Risk Surfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08933))

- HCAPO introduces hindsight credit assignment for long-horizon LLM agents by using the LLM itself as a post-hoc critic to assess how necessary each action was conditional on the final outcome and combining this with a multi-scale advantage that corrects misaligned baselines, yielding +7.7 percentage points success on WebShop and +13.8 on ALFWorld over GRPO for Qwen2.5‑7B‑Instruct agents. ([HCAPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08754))

- The Budget-Constrained Agentic Search (BCAS) harness measures how search depth, retrieval strategy, planning, reflection, and output token limits affect accuracy and cost in agentic RAG by gating tool calls against explicit per-query budgets; across six LLMs and three QA datasets, it finds accuracy gains saturate after a small number of searches, hybrid lexical+dense retrieval plus lightweight re-ranking provides the biggest boost, and larger completion budgets especially help HotpotQA-style multi-hop synthesis. ([BCAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08877))

- Niantic’s “living map” ambition is to maintain a continuously updated, machine-readable world model where each object carries semantic tags; as robots from partners like Coco and mapping firms like ESRI contribute sensor data, the maps become richer training grounds for embodied agents whose spatial understanding must approach human-level to coexist safely in shared spaces. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/03/10/1134099/how-pokemon-go-is-helping-robots-deliver-pizza-on-time/))

([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/open-data-for-ai))

- SPREAD tackles catastrophic forgetting in lifelong imitation learning by aligning teacher and student policy representations in low-rank subspaces via SVD-based “Subspace Representation Distillation” and adding a confidence-guided KL loss on top‑K most probable actions, significantly improving transfer and robustness over L2 feature matching baselines on the LIBERO benchmark. ([SPREAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08763))

## Retrieval, Reasoning, and Benchmarks on Structured Data

- SciTaRC is an expert-authored benchmark of questions over scientific tables, each with tables, gold answers, and pseudo-code plans; automatic evaluation with Llama‑3.3‑70B‑Instruct as judge shows GPT‑5 reaches only 76.8% accuracy, DeepSeek‑V3.2 (thinking) 73.6%, and open-weight Llama‑3.3‑70B‑Instruct just 34.5%, with analysis pointing to a universal “execution bottleneck” where models fail to faithfully carry out even correct high-level strategies on messy tables. ([SciTaRC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08910))

- SciTaRC’s complexity metrics quantify context load, structural size, number of tables used, arithmetic intensity, retrieval demand, plan length, and control-flow depth, and comparisons between chain-of-thought and program-of-thought methods show code-based approaches are brittle on heterogeneous tables while natural-language reasoning often fails at initial comprehension and arithmetic. ([SciTaRC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08910))

- NVIDIA’s SPEED‑Bench benchmark for speculative decoding uses diverse real-text sequences across 11 categories and input-length buckets (1K–32K tokens) to construct realistic throughput Pareto curves; internally it is already the main metric for Nemotron multi-token prediction performance, offering a standardized way to compare drafting strategies beyond random-token tests. ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/open-data-for-ai))

- Retrieval-Synthetic-NVDocs‑v1, built from 15K NVIDIA technical documents into 110K synthetic query–passage–answer triplets with varied reasoning types and query forms, enables rapid in-domain embedding and RAG fine-tuning—e.g., improving NDCG@10 by 11% for nvidia/llama‑nemotron‑embed‑1b‑v2 with ~2 hours of training on 8×A100. ([Hugging Face Blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/open-data-for-ai))

## Telecom, Edge AI, and Fairness

- The OWO-FMTL framework tackles fairness in AI-enabled Radio Access Networks where many users share an edge-hosted multi-task model; it uses an outer online loop to learn initializations per round and an inner primal–dual loop to adaptively reweight users’ tasks within each round according to generalized α‑fairness, achieving provable zero fairness regret under arbitrary, even adversarial, task sequences with low computation suitable for RAN deployment. ([Equitable Multi-Task Learning for AI-RANs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08717))

## Privacy, Anonymization, and Copyright

- MultiGraSCCo extends the German GraSCCo synthetic clinical corpus—with existing PHI annotations—by adding expert-annotated indirect personal identifiers (e.g., family, socioeconomic and criminal history, time, lifestyle) and high-quality machine translations into nine languages (Arabic, Italian, Russian, French, Turkish, Ukrainian, Persian, Polish, English) that preserve annotation spans and culturally adapt names and places, with medical professional evaluation confirming translation and identifier fidelity. ([MultiGraSCCo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08879))

- The authors release MultiGraSCCo and guidelines for use in training annotators, validating de-identification across institutions without legal entanglements, and benchmarking cross-lingual anonymization models, noting that GDPR’s re-identification risk standard makes robust treatment of indirect identifiers essential beyond HIPAA’s 18 PHI categories. ([MultiGraSCCo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08879))

- A German regional court held that lyrics remain copyrightable despite AI-generated backing music and rejected a defense claim that the text was purely AI-authored, finding that an expert’s stylistic analysis was insufficient to rebut the plaintiff’s sworn assertion of authorship and confirming an injunction against distribution and advertising of a song that reused substantial parts of her lyrics without permission. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/german-court-says-its-ai-isnt-enough-to-void-copyright/))

- The ruling suggests that future disputes over alleged AI authorship will hinge on concrete evidence of generation and documented human creative processes, not on generic arguments that stylistic quirks imply machine origin, raising the stakes for creators to record how they use AI tools. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/german-court-says-its-ai-isnt-enough-to-void-copyright/))

## Foundations, Representations, and Equivariance

- Using sparse autoencoders trained on Gemma models, “One Language, Two Scripts” shows that Gemma’s internal SAE features for Serbian sentences are highly script-invariant: cross-script identical sentences (Latin vs Cyrillic) achieve ~0.58 Jaccard overlap vs ~0.28 for random cross-script pairs and even cross-script paraphrases (~0.47) exceed random baselines, with invariance strengthening in larger models, indicating that high-level concepts are encoded above surface tokenization. ([One Language, Two Scripts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08869))

- The work proposes Serbian digraphia—two scripts with near-perfect character mapping but disjoint tokenization—as a controlled testbed for probing whether models’ learned “concepts” are tied to orthography or semantics, and finds script changes cause less representational divergence than within-script paraphrasing. ([One Language, Two Scripts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08869))

- “Generalized Reduction to the Isotropy” proves that when a group G acts transitively on a space M, any G-invariant on X×M can be reduced without loss of expressiveness to an invariant of the isotropy subgroup H (the stabilizer of a reference point in M) acting only on X, with an explicit orbit equivalence (X×M)/G ≅ X/H and constructive “canonicalization” maps that normalize the M component. ([Generalized Reduction to the Isotropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08758))

- Applying this reduction to Equivariant Neural Fields, the authors show how conditioning on homogeneous spaces can be handled via isotropy-group invariants, extending prior, more restrictive ENF frameworks to arbitrary group actions on arbitrary input spaces X and homogeneous conditioning spaces Z. ([Generalized Reduction to the Isotropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08758))

([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/better-code/#atom-everything))

- OpenAI has enabled interactive math and science explanations for more than 70 high school and college-level concepts in ChatGPT—such as binomial squares, exponential decay, Ohm’s law, compound interest, and trig identities—where users can adjust variables and immediately see corresponding changes in graphs and formulas, with access available to all logged-in users worldwide regardless of subscription tier. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-now-explains-math-and-physics-with-interactive-visualizations/))
