### **US AI Governance, Defense, and Contract Power**

- The Trump administration’s draft GSA guidance for civilian AI contracts would force vendors to grant the US government an irrevocable license to use AI systems for “all lawful use,” ban models from making ideological/partisan judgments (including favoring diversity programs), and require disclosure of any model changes made to comply with non‑US rules like the EU DSA. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/trump-administration-drafts-ai-contract-rules-requiring-companies-to-license-systems-for-all-lawful-use/))  
- The guidance follows the Pentagon’s cancellation of a $200M Anthropic contract after Anthropic insisted on prohibitions against domestic mass surveillance and autonomous weapons; Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth accused Anthropic of seeking “veto power” over military decisions, and the White House labeled the company a supply‑chain risk. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/trump-administration-drafts-ai-contract-rules-requiring-companies-to-license-systems-for-all-lawful-use/))  
- OpenAI has now signed a DoD deal including technical safeguards that bar domestic mass surveillance and require human responsibility for use of force—including autonomous weapons—and CEO Sam Altman urged the Pentagon to offer the same terms to all AI vendors. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-820-gpt-54-cursor))  
- The Pentagon formally designated Anthropic a supply‑chain risk, prohibiting defense contractors from using Claude, but continues to use Claude in Iran‑related military operations because it’s deeply embedded in classified systems; Anthropic plans to challenge the designation in court, and Microsoft will keep offering Claude to non‑defense customers. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-820-gpt-54-cursor))

### **Citation Integrity, Evaluation Tools, and AI Self‑Detection**

- Peer‑reviewed papers at leading AI conferences, including NeurIPS, ACL, and ICLR, have been found to contain numerous hallucinated citations—references to non‑existent papers constructed by LLMs that undermine reproducibility and co‑author integrity. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/hallucinated-references-are-passing-peer-review-at-top-ai-conferences-and-a-new-open-tool-wants-to-fix-that/))  
- To address this, researchers released CiteAudit, an open‑source benchmark and multi‑agent detection system trained on 6,475 real and 2,967 fake citations from LLMs (GPT, Gemini, Claude, Qwen, Llama) and real‑world hallucinations from Google Scholar, OpenReview, arXiv, and bioRxiv. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/hallucinated-references-are-passing-peer-review-at-top-ai-conferences-and-a-new-open-tool-wants-to-fix-that/))  
- CiteAudit uses five specialized agents (extractor, memory, web‑search, judge, scholar) running Qwen3‑VL‑235B locally to verify references, achieving 97.2% overall accuracy, identifying all 467 forged citations in real papers while mislabeling only 100 of 2,889 genuine ones, and processing ~10 references in 2.3 seconds. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/hallucinated-references-are-passing-peer-review-at-top-ai-conferences-and-a-new-open-tool-wants-to-fix-that/))  
- In the same benchmark, GPT‑5.2, GPTZero, and Gemini 3 Pro either missed many real‑world fakes or falsely flagged ~45–50% of legitimate citations as hallucinated, and often performed opaque, non‑traceable “searches” even when prompted to use external tools. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/hallucinated-references-are-passing-peer-review-at-top-ai-conferences-and-a-new-open-tool-wants-to-fix-that/))  
- CiteAudit is available as a free web app (up to 500 citations/day per email), with higher limits available via user‑supplied Gemini API keys. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/hallucinated-references-are-passing-peer-review-at-top-ai-conferences-and-a-new-open-tool-wants-to-fix-that/))  
- A separate NewsGuard investigation cited in the same piece found that leading chatbots including ChatGPT, Gemini, and Grok often failed to recognize AI‑generated videos from OpenAI’s Sora and instead provided confident but incorrect explanations and fabricated news sources. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/hallucinated-references-are-passing-peer-review-at-top-ai-conferences-and-a-new-open-tool-wants-to-fix-that/))  

### **AI Research Highlights**

- “Beyond Language Modeling: An Exploration of Multimodal Pretraining” (FAIR/Meta/NYU) uses the Transfusion framework to jointly pretrain vision and language, finding representation autoencoders are an optimal visual backbone and that vision requires substantially more data than language for comparable performance. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-820-gpt-54-cursor))  
- DREAM (MIT CSAIL & Meta) presents a unified model that jointly optimizes discriminative and generative objectives for vision, leveraging “Masking Warmup” and “Semantically Aligned Decoding” to improve both image understanding and text‑to‑image generation fidelity. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-820-gpt-54-cursor))  
- Databricks’ KARL (“Knowledge Agents via Reinforcement Learning”) trains enterprise search agents with multi‑task off‑policy RL and an agentic synthesis pipeline, reaching state‑of‑the‑art grounded reasoning on complex search tasks while improving cost‑efficiency and cross‑domain generalization versus leading closed models. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-820-gpt-54-cursor))  
- Meta’s “Agentic Code Reasoning” study introduces a semi‑formal reasoning approach—structured prompting templates that let LLM agents reason over codebases without execution, significantly boosting accuracy on patch equivalence and fault‑localization tasks. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-820-gpt-54-cursor))  
- “Bayesian Teaching Enables Probabilistic Reasoning in Large Language Models” (MIT, Meta, Google DeepMind, Google Research) shows that fine‑tuning LLMs to imitate a normative Bayesian teacher markedly improves their ability to update probabilistic beliefs over multi‑turn interactions and generalizes to unseen tasks and preference‑learning scenarios. ([TheSequence](https://thesequence.substack.com/p/the-sequence-radar-820-gpt-54-cursor))

### **Human Behavior & Evolutionary Strategy**

- A University of Chieti‑Pescara study of ~1,100 participants found that people with stronger left‑handedness scored higher on development‑oriented competitiveness and lower on anxiety‑driven avoidance of competition than right‑handers, and strongly lateralized left‑handers showed elevated “hypercompetitiveness” (a strong desire to win even at others’ expense). ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/left-handed-people-are-more-competitive-says-science/))  
- In a pegboard dexterity test on a 48‑person subsample, there were no significant differences in motor skills between left‑ and right‑handers, and no correlations between handedness and Big Five traits or baseline depression/anxiety, suggesting the competitiveness effect is specific rather than a general personality shift. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/left-handed-people-are-more-competitive-says-science/))  
- The findings support an “evolutionarily stable strategy” account: a minority of left‑handers gains a frequency‑dependent advantage in one‑on‑one competitive contexts (e.g., sports) due to being less predictable, maintaining a stable ~10% prevalence across generations despite a right‑handed cooperative majority. ([WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/left-handed-people-are-more-competitive-says-science/))
