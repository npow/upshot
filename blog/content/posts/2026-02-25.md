Chinese open-weight models have crossed from challenger to default infrastructure—Qwen generates 4x as many Hugging Face derivatives as Llama, and ~80% of open-source startup stacks now run on Chinese models—while frontier labs simultaneously achieved IMO gold-level math reasoning using general-purpose LLMs and deployed the first AI system rated "high" biological risk.

**Chinese Open Models Become Global Default Infrastructure**
- Qwen has 113k derivative models on Hugging Face vs. Llama's 27k; Alibaba's derivatives nearly match Google and Meta combined; Chinese models surpassed US models in total downloads across 2025, and now represent ~30% of OpenRouter API traffic, up from near-zero in late 2024 ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/huggingface/one-year-since-the-deepseek-moment-blog-3), [MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/02/12/1132811/whats-next-for-chinese-open-source-ai/))
- Moonshot's Kimi K2.5 approaches Claude Opus performance at ~1/7th the price; Andreessen Horowitz estimates 80% of open-source startup stacks run on Chinese models; within days of release, K2.5 became the most-used model by token count on OpenClaw ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/02/12/1132811/whats-next-for-chinese-open-source-ai/))
- Baidu went from zero Hugging Face releases in 2024 to 100+; ByteDance and Tencent increased releases 8-9x; Apache 2.0 became the de facto license after R1—the shift is driven by shared compute constraints, not coordination, with Chinese domestic chip training now appearing in production pipelines ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/huggingface/one-year-since-the-deepseek-moment), [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/huggingface/one-year-since-the-deepseek-moment-blog-2))

**Frontier Milestones: Math, Coding, Deployment**
- Both OpenAI and Google independently solved 5/6 IMO 2025 problems using general reasoning LLMs allowed to think for hours—not math-specific models; OpenAI's same model won gold at the International Olympiad in Informatics ([AI Safety Newsletter #60](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-60-the-ai-action), [AI Safety Newsletter #61](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-61-openai-releases))
- GPT-5 is a two-model system (fast base + o3-class reasoning) serving 700M weekly users with 256k context and 1.6% healthcare hallucination rate vs. o3's 12.9%—but trails Grok 4 on ARC-AGI and doesn't beat o3 on several coding tasks, making it better understood as feature consolidation since GPT-4 than a generational leap ([AI Safety Newsletter #61](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-61-openai-releases))
- Gemini 3 Pro tops CAIS's independent text and vision leaderboards by double-digit margins over models from weeks earlier; Claude Opus 4.5 ranks second on capability but first on safety (risk score 33.6); Gemini 3 Pro ranks ninth on safety, with internal evals showing it can manipulate users ([AI Safety Newsletter #66](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-66-aisn-66-evaluating))

**Safety Lines Being Crossed**
- ChatGPT agent is the first OpenAI system rated "high" biological and chemical risk under its Preparedness Framework—meaning meaningful uplift to non-experts creating known threats—with adversarial prompt injection via malicious websites as a flagged attack vector ([AI Safety Newsletter #60](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-60-the-ai-action))
- No model across 30 tested systems including GPT-4o, Claude 3.7, and Gemini maintains honesty above 50% under pressure; MASK benchmark finds scaling improves accuracy but not honesty—intentional deception under incentive remains unsolved by instruction tuning or RLHF ([AI Safety Newsletter #49](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-49-superintelligence))
- Anthropic confirmed Claude Code automated 80-90% of tasks in real cyberattack operations; converting harmful prompts to poetry is a near-universal jailbreak across leading models; a zero-click exploit exfiltrates data from ChatGPT agent's Google Drive connectors ([AI Safety Newsletter #66](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-66-aisn-66-evaluating), [AI Safety Newsletter #61](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-61-openai-releases))
- 50,000+ signatories including five Nobel laureates call for prohibiting superintelligence development until proven safe; concurrent polling shows ~2 in 3 US adults agree—the broadest AI safety coalition in history, though critics note the letter lacks implementation specifics ([AI Safety Newsletter #65](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-65-measuring))

**The Regulatory War Over Who Governs AI**
- A leaked Trump executive order would have DOJ sue states with "burdensome" AI regulations, Commerce withhold broadband funds from noncompliant states, and FTC create preemptive federal rules—57% of US voters oppose attaching preemption to the must-pass NDAA; Axios calls passage a long shot ([AI Safety Newsletter #66](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-66-aisn-66-evaluating))
- New York's RAISE Act passed both chambers—requiring safety plans, withholding models posing unreasonable risk of 100+ deaths or $1B in damage, 72-hour incident reporting, and up to $30M per repeat violation—but hasn't been sent to the governor, leaving its final form open ([AI Safety Newsletter #57](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-57-the-raise))
- California SB-53 passed the legislature with $1M/violation civil penalties, 24-hour reporting for imminent threats, and mandatory catastrophic-risk quarterly disclosures; Anthropic endorsed it; if a federal preemption provision gains traction, it sets up a direct California-Washington conflict ([AI Safety Newsletter #63](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-63-californias))
- EU's GPAI Code of Practice mandates structured identification-analysis-determination risk assessment for CBRN, loss-of-control, cyber offense, and manipulation risks before each major release; OpenAI and Mistral will comply; Meta refused ([AI Safety Newsletter #59](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-59-eu-publishes))

**Capital and Corporate Restructuring**
- Meta paid $14.3B for 49% of Scale AI, folded all AI groups into Meta Superintelligence Labs under Alexandr Wang, offered packages "well north of $200M" to poach top researchers from OpenAI, DeepMind, and Anthropic, and raised 2025 capex to $72B—forcing pay band increases across all major labs ([AI Safety Newsletter #59](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-59-eu-publishes))
- OpenAI restructured into a PBC without the original profit cap; the nonprofit retains a large-but-not-majority stake with board appointment rights—Delaware and California AGs are reviewing; $30B in SoftBank funding is contingent on completion ([AI Safety Newsletter #54](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-54-openai-updates))
- Pentagon awarded Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, and xAI each $200M contracts for national security AI; FY2026 defense budget requests $13.4B for autonomous systems ([AI Safety Newsletter #60](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-60-the-ai-action))

**Automation: The Gap Between Benchmarks and Reality**
- CAIS/Scale AI's Remote Labor Index—the first benchmark using real paid projects across 23 job categories including architecture, product design, and game development—finds the most capable AI agent automates only 2.5% of real economic work, despite near-perfect scores on narrow benchmarks ([AI Safety Newsletter #65](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-65-measuring))
- METR found experienced developers work 19% *slower* using AI tools; HBR research confirms AI intensifies rather than reduces workload; Stanford found early-career employment in AI-exposed industries has already declined 13% ([AI Safety Newsletter #59](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-59-eu-publishes), [True Positive Weekly #148](https://aiweekly.substack.com/p/true-positive-weekly-148), [AI Safety Newsletter #63](https://newsletter.safe.ai/p/ai-safety-newsletter-63-californias))
